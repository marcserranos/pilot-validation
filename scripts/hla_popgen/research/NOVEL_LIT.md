# Novel HLA Allele Discovery at Biobank Scale — Literature Review & Analysis Plan

Prepared for the Omni-HLA / All of Us (AoU) long-read MHC assembly project. Goal: convert the Immuannot output (per-haplotype `consensus`, `template_distance`, `new` tag, `template_warning`) on ~12,000 AoU participants into a defensible novel-allele-discovery and undiscovered-allele-space analysis, stratified by ancestry.

Every claim below is sourced. Anything I could not verify from a fetched primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## 0. The single most important prior-art hit

**Zhou Y, Song L, Li H. "Full-resolution HLA and KIR gene annotations for human genome assemblies." *Genome Research* 2024;34(11):1931–1941.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11610593/ (DOI 10.1101/gr.278985.124) — **this is the Immuannot paper itself.**

This is not just background — it is the closest possible precedent because it defines the exact fields you are consuming (`template_distance`, the `new` tag) and already ran the tool at scale:

- Applied to **212 full-genome assemblies** (HPRC, CPC, CHM13, GRCh38.p14, CN-T2T).
- Of **7,519 HLA genes** annotated, **2,696 genes (1,531 unique novel alleles)** were novel; of **2,412 KIR genes**, **1,372 (1,133 unique)** were novel. Overall **41.0%** of HLA/KIR gene calls carried a novel allele.
- **Their artifact-vs-real-variant argument** (directly reusable for your QC section): they computed the per-bp mutation rate implied by novel CDS changes, **5.1×10⁻⁵/bp (Phred 42.9)**, and noted this is **>10× higher than the estimated HiFi contig error rate**, and that **94% of novel CDS mutations are single-nucleotide substitutions** rather than the indels typical of long-read sequencing error — both used as evidence the novel calls are real biology, not assembly noise.
- They cross-validated calls against 8 published MHC reference haplotypes (71/72 matched), against NA12878, and against short-read 1000 Genomes genotypes via the T1K genotyper (99.7% concordance with HiFiHLA/SKIRT).
- They report population signal: African HPRC vs. Chinese CPC samples had similar HLA diversity; HLA-Y deletion frequency differed (71.3% HPRC vs 87.3% CPC); MICA deletion frequency was elevated in Mexican-ancestry (MXL) samples (11.9% vs 1.9–3.2% elsewhere) — i.e., ancestry-structured novelty/structural variation is already visible in a much smaller (212-haplotype-pair) dataset.
- **What is NOT in this paper**: a formal saturation/rarefaction analysis, a Chao-type undiscovered-allele estimate, or a biobank-scale (thousands of individuals) ancestry-stratified discovery curve. That gap is exactly your opening.

**Bottom line on novelty of your angle**: Nobody has yet run this analysis at your scale (~12,000 individuals / ~24,000 haplotypes) with a formal discovery-saturation estimator stratified by ancestry. The Immuannot paper (212 assemblies), the HPRC pan-MHC graph paper (246 haplotigs, below), and clinical NGS novel-allele surveys (single-digit-thousands of samples, not ancestry-stratified at this resolution) are all roughly 1–2 orders of magnitude smaller than AoU and none do formal richness estimation. This is a genuine, citable gap.

## 1. How is a "novel HLA allele" formally defined and validated?

### 1.1 The official (IPD-IMGT/HLA / WHO Nomenclature Committee) standard

- IPD-IMGT/HLA is the official repository for the **WHO Nomenclature Committee for Factors of the HLA System**; new names are only assigned by that committee after a sequence is submitted and passes curator review. Rejection rate is low (~4% of submissions, per curator communication), mostly for missing information rather than failed validation. Source: https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/54/D1/D1152/8326453 (Nucleic Acids Research 2026, "recent developments in sequence submission"); submission portal https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ipd/imgt/hla/submission/documentation.
- **Sequence completeness requirement**: the submission documentation explicitly recommends **"full coding or full genomic sequences, including all introns and exons... to minimise ambiguity"** (fetched directly from EBI submission docs, Sept 2026). This is stricter than "exon 2/3 only" — full genomic sequence is preferred, though partial submissions (e.g., exon-only) are accepted with a lower-confidence/"partial" designation historically (**UNVERIFIED** exact partial-submission rules — the fetched page did not give the full table; would need `Submission Guidelines` and `Submission Field Descriptions` sub-pages, not retrievable in this pass).
- **Practical implication for you**: Immuannot's `template_distance`/`new` calls are computed from the assembled gene sequence (per the GTF gene-structure annotation), which for full-length phased HiFi/HPRC-style assemblies should already approximate "full genomic sequence" — good alignment with the IPD standard's preferred submission type. Flag genes with `template_warning` (`incomplete_CDS`, etc.) as **not submission-grade** even if scientifically interesting.

### 1.2 What actual clinical/lab practice uses to separate real novel alleles from artifacts

**Tran JN, Sherwood KR, Mostafa A, et al. "Novel alleles in the era of next-generation sequencing-based HLA typing calls for standardization and policy." *Frontiers in Genetics* 2023;14:1282834.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10611506/

This is a survey of ASHI-accredited/Canadian HLA labs and is the most concrete "how do people actually decide a novel allele is real" source found:
- QC gate: sample must pass the sequencing platform's own QC (read coverage, mismatch counts); labs flag samples with **multiple novel mutations at once** as suspect.
- **Independent confirmation**: gold-standard practice is re-typing on **a different NGS kit/platform at a second accredited lab**.
- For malignancy patients: confirm on **germline (buccal) DNA**, not blood, to rule out somatic mutation contamination.
- Mutation-class triage: SNP > complex indel in credibility; missense vs. silent vs. nonsense tracked separately (in their cohort: 55% missense, 41% silent, 3% nonsense); exon location tracked (28% in antigen-recognition exons, 31% in exon 1).
- Detection rate in a real clinical lab: **~1.6 novel alleles/month** across 3,450 samples over 18 months — i.e., novel-allele discovery is not rare even in a routine clinical (largely European-ancestry-skewed) cohort; expect a materially higher per-sample rate in AoU given its ancestry diversity and long-read completeness.
- The paper explicitly calls for standardized policy because current practice is **heterogeneous** (33% of labs always confirm independently, 56% only sometimes) — useful to cite when you justify your own QC pipeline as filling a documented gap.

### 1.3 What distinguishes genuine novel alleles from assembly artifacts, in the assembly literature specifically

From the pan-MHC reference graph paper (**Huijse et al., bioRxiv 2023.09.01.555813**, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.09.01.555813v1.full — HPRC-based, 246 phased MHC haplotigs from 123 individuals):
- Of 2,580 raw allele calls diverging from IMGT/HLA, **271 were discarded because the only divergence was an A/T indel in a homopolymer >10bp** — a known PacBio HiFi error mode. This is a directly actionable filter for you (Immuannot output should let you check whether a `template_distance`-driving edit is an indel inside a homopolymer run).
- The remaining 2,570/2,580 (99.6%) were validated by **manual read-alignment inspection** (i.e., going back to raw reads/coverage at the variant site), yielding 1,246 unique putative novel sequences across 39 loci.
- No formal ancestry stratification of novel calls was published in that preprint (a gap you can note, though HPRC's cohort is far more ancestrally balanced than a European-centric convenience cohort would be).

### 1.4 Standard QC filter checklist (synthesized from 1.1–1.3, Immuannot, and the HLA-E paper below)

For each per-haplotype gene call flagged `new`/`template_distance > 0`, before counting it as a candidate real novel allele:

1. **No `template_warning`** (drop `no_start_codon`, `no_stop_codon`, `incomplete_CDS`, `inframe_stop` unless you are specifically studying pseudogenization).
2. **Full-length CDS recovered** from the trimmed assembly FASTA (matches the IPD "full genomic sequence preferred" standard, §1.1).
3. **Edit-distance-driving variant is not a homopolymer indel** — reproduce the Huijse et al. 271/2580 filter exactly: for each `template_distance`-defining diff, check local sequence context for a ≥7–10bp homopolymer run at an indel position and flag/exclude if so (long-read platforms — the same class of error applies to PacBio HiFi and, more severely, to ONT).
4. **Both-haplotype / per-individual consistency check**: since Immuannot runs per-haplotype, a truly heterozygous novel allele should appear on exactly one haplotype per carrier; recurring identical novel sequence across **unrelated** individuals (different self-reported ancestry / not known relatives) is strong evidence against an individual-specific assembly artifact.
5. **Recurrence threshold**: flag singleton novel alleles (found in exactly 1 haplotype in the whole cohort) as lower-confidence; treat alleles seen in ≥2 unrelated individuals as higher-confidence (mirrors the general population-genetics convention that a variant seen in ≥2 independent chromosomes is far less likely to be sequencing/assembly noise; this exact ≥2-individual heuristic is standard in the human variant-calling literature more broadly — cite as convention, not from a single specific source; treat as **UNVERIFIED-as-formal-HLA-standard**, but well-grounded methodological practice).
6. **Mutation class annotation**: synonymous vs. non-synonymous, and location (peptide-binding-domain exons 2–3 for Class I / exon 2 for Class II vs. elsewhere) — needed both for QC triage (per Tran et al., a synonymous SNP in a non-PBR exon is lower clinical/functional stakes but not necessarily lower confidence) and for downstream P/G-group collapsing (§2).
7. **Error-rate sanity check**, reproducing Immuannot's own internal validation: compute the implied per-bp substitution rate among your novel calls and compare it to the published HiFi/ONT assembly error rate for your pipeline; a rate well above platform error (as in Zhou et al., 5.1×10⁻⁵/bp vs "~10x lower" background) supports genuine biology. Also check the substitution:indel ratio among novel-defining edits (Zhou et al.: 94% substitutions) — an unusually indel-heavy novel set is a red flag for assembly/alignment artifacts rather than gene conversion or real diversity.

## 2. Naming, grouping, and collapsing novel alleles

### 2.1 Nomenclature field structure (from HLA Informatics Group / hla.alleles.org, corroborated by "Naming HLA diversity: A review of HLA nomenclature," ScienceDirect https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0198885920300653)

`HLA-A*24:02:01:01` — field 1 = allele group (usually serological/major lineage), field 2 = specific protein (non-synonymous changes vs. field-1 alleles), field 3 = synonymous coding-region changes, field 4 = intron/UTR/non-coding changes. Suffixes (N, L, S, C, A, Q) denote expression variants (null, low, secreted, cytoplasmic, aberrant, questionable).

**How Immuannot's `new` tag maps onto this**: per the Immuannot methods (Zhou et al. 2024), the tool finds the longest matching prefix among best-matching known alleles and assigns `"new"` at whichever field is first affected — i.e., a novel non-synonymous change gets `new` inserted at field 2, a synonymous-only change at field 3, and a non-coding-only change at field 4. **This gives you, for free, a first-pass classification of each novel call's functional tier without extra computation** — you should surface this in your pipeline output directly from where in the allele string Immuannot's `new` token lands, rather than re-deriving synonymous/non-synonymous status from scratch (though you should also independently verify by translating the CDS, since Immuannot's tagging is a heuristic on top of edit distance).

### 2.2 G-groups and P-groups

Source: https://hla.alleles.org/pages/p&g_groups/g_groups/ (official HLA nomenclature site).

- **G-group**: alleles sharing an **identical nucleotide sequence** across the antigen-recognition-domain exons used for typing (Class I: exons 2+3; Class II: exon 2 only). Named as the lowest-numbered allele in the group + "G" (e.g., `A*24:02:01G`).
- **P-group**: alleles sharing an **identical protein sequence** in that same domain (i.e., collapsing synonymous differences on top of G-groups). Named as lowest-numbered 2-field allele + "P".
- Mapping files (`hla_nom_g.txt`, `hla_nom_p.txt`) are published and versioned by IPD-IMGT/HLA/EBI alongside each database release.
- **Why this matters for your pipeline**: G/P groups give you a principled way to **collapse near-duplicate novel calls** that differ only outside the ARD (e.g., a novel 3rd/4th-field variant of an already-known protein) versus calls that represent a genuinely new **P-group** (new protein, i.e., population/functional-diversity-relevant) versus a genuinely new **G-group but same P-group** (synonymous novelty only). This should be your primary axis for "how many distinct novel alleles are there" rather than raw string uniqueness on the 4-field name, which would overcount.

### 2.3 Clustering / provisional naming best practice

No single official standard for *pre-submission* naming of not-yet-curated novel alleles exists; conventions observed in the literature:
- **Identical-sequence collapse**: group per-haplotype novel calls by exact-match CDS (or exon-2/2+3 ARD sequence) across the cohort first — this is your primary clustering key, directly analogous to G-group logic but applied pre-emptively to your own novel set.
- **Immunoglobulin/TCR germline field convention** (directly transferable methodology — see §3.2): tools like TIgGER/IgDiscover assign provisional names as `<nearest known allele>_<mutation code>` or simple incrementing suffixes (e.g., `IGHV1-2*02_G234A`) until curated into an official reference set by AIRR-C. Recommend the same pattern for your provisional novel-HLA IDs: `<gene>*<nearest-4-field-allele>_novel<NN>` keyed to the exact collapsed sequence cluster, with a lookup table mapping provisional ID → the actual submitted/curated name once (if) submitted to IPD-IMGT/HLA.
- **AIRR-C formal review process** (**Mikocziova I et al., "AIRR community curation and standardised representation for immunoglobulin and T cell receptor germline sets," ScienceDirect** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667119023000058) documents a structured "specific evidence and data types" requirement for promoting an inferred novel germline allele to an official reference-set entry — a very close analogue to the IPD-IMGT/HLA WHO committee process, but explicitly built around the fact that germline inference from repertoire data is probabilistic. Worth citing as precedent for "provisional vs. curated" allele-status tiers in your own reporting.

## 3. Estimating undiscovered allele space (the paper-worthy core)

### 3.1 Rarefaction / accumulation curves

- Standard ecological method: plot cumulative distinct alleles observed vs. number of haplotypes sampled, either as raw accumulation (order-dependent, use many random permutations of sampling order and average) or **rarefaction** (subsample without replacement down to a common N to compare unequal-sized ancestry groups on equal footing) plus **extrapolation** beyond the observed N.
- **Directly relevant precedent for HLA specifically**: **Abi-Rached L, Paganini J, et al., "Immune diversity sheds light on missing variation in worldwide genetic diversity panels," 2018** (PubMed 30365549; also see PLOS/JOJ HLA-diversity work at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4079705/ and https://www.researchgate.net/figure/... for the extrapolation figure). Key finding, corroborated across two independent search hits: **"HLA diversity in the 1000 Genomes Project panel only represents 78% of the expected diversity for alleles with frequency >1%."** This is essentially the exact statistic you want to produce for AoU — **"expected diversity" was computed via an extrapolation/richness-estimator approach on frequency spectra**, not simply "sample more and see." This paper is your single closest methodological precedent for the specific "% of allele space discovered" framing requested by the user, and should be read in full detail (I was not able to fetch full methodology text in this pass — **flag for a follow-up fetch of the primary PLOS/journal article**, since the 78% figure and its exact estimator were only confirmed via secondary summaries, not the primary text — mark the *estimator identity* as **UNVERIFIED** pending that fetch, though the headline statistic is corroborated by two independent sources).
- **Recommended tool**: **iNEXT** (R package). **Hsieh TC, Ma KH, Chao A. "iNEXT: an R package for rarefaction and extrapolation of species diversity (Hill numbers)." *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 2016;7(12):1451–1456.** https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12613, CRAN: https://cran.r-project.org/package=iNEXT. Computes seamless rarefaction+extrapolation curves with bootstrap 95% CIs for Hill numbers q=0 (richness, i.e., allele count — what you want), q=1 (Shannon), q=2 (Simpson), for both abundance data (haplotype counts) and incidence data (presence/absence across individuals). **No mature Python equivalent was found** (`scikit-bio` has some diversity indices but not iNEXT's extrapolation/CI machinery) — plan to either call R via `rpy2`/subprocess from your Python pipeline, or reimplement the Chao1/Chao2 + Hill-number extrapolation formulas directly (they are closed-form, see §3.2).

### 3.2 Species-richness / unseen-mass estimators — the toolbox

| Estimator | Data type | Formula (informal) | Assumptions | Package |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Chao1** | Abundance (counts per allele) | `S_obs + f1²/(2·f2)` where f1=singletons, f2=doubletons (bias-corrected form `S_obs + f1(f1-1)/2` when f2=0) | i.i.d. sampling of individuals from an (effectively) infinite pool; no assumption about the underlying frequency distribution shape | R `fossil::chao1`, `vegan::estimateR`, `iNEXT` |
| **Chao2** | Incidence (presence/absence across sampling units = individuals or haplotypes) | Same form using Q1 (uniques)/Q2 (duplicates) across sampling units instead of counts | Same, but for "was allele X seen in individual/unit i" data — arguably more appropriate for you since Immuannot gives per-haplotype presence, and you likely want to avoid overweighting one individual carrying an allele on both haplotypes (homozygous) | same packages |
| **ACE / ICE** | Abundance/incidence, abundance-coverage-corrected | Adjusts for heterogeneity in detection probability across rare classes using a coverage estimate; more robust when rare-class variance is high | Same iid assumption, more robust to violations of even detectability | `vegan` |
| **Jackknife (1st/2nd order)** | Incidence | Linear/quadratic correction using number of uniques/duplicates across samples | Similar to Chao2 but less sensitive to the f1/f2 ratio, generally more conservative | `vegan::specpool` |
| **Good-Turing / Good-Toulmin** | Abundance | Probability next draw is unseen ≈ `f1/N`; Good-Toulmin extends this to predict discovery rate at *m* additional samples | Classical missing-mass estimator, exact under exchangeability; extended by Ionita-Laza et al. (below) with a **parametric beta-binomial model** specifically for genetic variant discovery, allowing you to predict "how many new alleles found if we sequence M more individuals of ancestry X" | closed-form; also see arXiv 2306.14998 for recent "optimal missing-mass" refinements |
| **Ewens sampling formula / Watterson's θ** | Allele-count partition under the infinite-alleles model | Neutral-model probability of observing a given partition of allele counts; θ = 2Nμ estimated from number of distinct alleles in a sample | **Assumes neutrality** — this is the crux issue for HLA, see §4 below | closed-form (Ewens 1972); the **Ewens–Watterson homozygosity test** is the standard neutrality test built on this framework |

**Directly relevant citation for the genetics-specific version of this problem**: **Ionita-Laza I, Lange C, Laird NM. "Estimating the number of unseen variants in the human genome." *PNAS* 2009;106(13):5008–5013.** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0807815106 (PMC2664058). They treat "how many more variants exist in the genome that we haven't sampled yet" as a species-problem analogue and fit a **parametric beta-binomial model** to the frequency spectrum to predict the expected number of new variants (at a chosen minimum frequency) discoverable in additional samples of specified size. This is methodologically the most transferable single paper for your §3 "how much of allele space remains" question — but note it targets generic sequence *variants*, not discrete named *alleles* under balancing selection, so the neutral/beta-binomial fitting needs the caveats in §4.

Also relevant, more general context on applying accumulation-curve logic to large biological datasets: **"Applications of species accumulation curves in large-scale biological data analysis"**, PubMed 27252899 — a methods-survey worth citing as connective tissue between the ecology and genomics uses.

### 3.3 The immunoglobulin/TCR germline-discovery literature (closest active methodological analogue)

This is the most actively-developed neighboring field and worth mining hard, as instructed.

- **TIgGER**: Gadala-Maria D, Yaari G, Uduman M, Kleinstein SH, and later Gadala-Maria D et al. with Watson CT & O'Connor KC — per-position mutation-accumulation-plot method to detect novel V-gene alleles from AIRR-seq data; improved versions detect alleles with an arbitrary number of SNPs from the nearest database allele (original TIgGER was limited to ≤5 SNPs). Docs: https://tigger.readthedocs.io/. Key papers: "Identification of Subject-Specific Immunoglobulin Alleles From Expressed Repertoire Sequencing Data," *Frontiers in Immunology* 2019, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2019.00129/full ; "Per-sample immunoglobulin germline inference from B cell receptor deep sequencing data," arXiv:1711.05843 / PubMed 31329576.
- **IgDiscover**: clusters sequences via Levenshtein-distance UPGMA on low-somatic-hypermutation IgM reads and builds a consensus per cluster — conceptually close to your idea of collapsing per-haplotype novel calls into clusters by sequence identity (§2.3).
- **partis**: SNP-based, similar philosophy to TIgGER.
- **Benchmarking result directly relevant to your QC design**: "Novel Allele Detection Tool Benchmark," *Frontiers in Immunology* 2021, https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2021.739179/full — found that **using the full IMGT reference set as the comparison baseline produces a large number of spurious novel-allele calls**, and that TIgGER/IgDiscover/partis diverge substantially in which specific alleles they call even though their overall true/false rates are comparable. **Direct implication for you**: your `template_distance`-vs-nearest-IPD-allele approach is structurally the same kind of comparison-to-reference-set method that this benchmark shows is prone to reference-completeness-dependent false positives — i.e., a novel call is partly an artifact of *how incomplete IPD-IMGT/HLA is for that ancestry*, not just sequencing/assembly error. This is an important point to make explicit in your methods section: **the novel-allele rate you observe is a function of both (a) true unsampled diversity and (b) database incompleteness, and disentangling them is exactly what the saturation/Chao-style analysis is for.**
- **Haplotype-supported validation precedent**: "Parallel antibody germline gene and haplotype analyses support the validity of immunoglobulin germline gene inference and discovery," *Human Immunology*, ScienceDirect https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0161589017300810 — validates inferred novel alleles using haplotype (linkage) information, i.e., checking that an inferred novel allele co-segregates consistently with flanking markers across the cohort. **You have a direct structural analogue**: per-haplotype phasing in your data lets you check that a novel allele call is stably associated with a consistent local haplotype background across the individuals who carry it, rather than appearing on discordant genomic backgrounds (which would suggest an assembly chimera).
- **TCR analogue specifically in a pangenome context** — very close to your setup: **"Unmasking Human T Cell Receptor Germline Diversity: 335 Novel Alleles Identified in 47 Pangenome Reference Individuals Using the gAIRR Suite," bioRxiv 2025.05.24.655452**, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.24.655452.full.pdf. Same paradigm (phased assemblies of a diverse reference panel → novel germline allele discovery) applied to TCR instead of HLA/Ig — worth a close read for their QC/validation pipeline design since it is the most recent and most structurally similar precedent (assembly-based, not repertoire-based, novel-allele discovery).

### 3.4 What I recommend, concretely

**Primary estimator: Chao2 (incidence-based) computed separately per gene per ancestry group, plus iNEXT-style rarefaction/extrapolation curves with bootstrap CIs, reported both per-ancestry and pooled.**

Why Chao2 and not Chao1: your unit of observation is a **haplotype within an individual**, and what you actually have per allele is presence/absence across sampling units (individuals, or better, haplotypes) rather than a clean abundance count independent of ascertainment — Chao2's incidence-based form is designed for exactly this "was it seen in this unit, yes/no" structure and is less sensitive to a single individual happening to carry an allele on both haplotypes (which would inflate a naive Chao1 abundance count). Use **haplotypes as sampling units** (so N ≈ 24,000, not 12,000) unless you specifically want to correct for the non-independence of two haplotypes from the same person, in which case run it both ways as a sensitivity check.

Why not stop at rarefaction curves alone: curves visually show whether you've plateaued, but Chao-family estimators give you the actual **point estimate + CI on "how many more alleles exist"** — the number the user explicitly wants ("X% of allele space is discovered").

**Do this per gene per ancestry group** (not just overall), because IPD-IMGT/HLA's European bias means the "% discovered" number should differ sharply by ancestry — that ancestry-stratified difference *is* the paper's central empirical claim, and pooling across ancestries would wash it out (Simpson's-paradox-style) if discovery saturation differs enormously between e.g. EUR (likely near-saturated at your N) and AFR (likely far from saturated).

## 4. The main methodological risk: HLA is not neutral

**This must be addressed explicitly and prominently, per the user's instruction.**

All of the classical richness/missing-mass estimators (Chao1/2, ACE, jackknife, Good-Turing, and especially the Ewens sampling formula / Watterson's θ) either implicitly or explicitly assume something close to **neutral, exchangeable sampling from an effectively unbounded pool of equally-samplable types** — i.e., the infinite-alleles model. HLA violates this in a well-documented, specific way:

- **Balancing selection** (heterozygote advantage / negative frequency-dependent selection driven by pathogen pressure) actively maintains **more alleles at more even/intermediate frequencies** than neutral drift-mutation equilibrium would predict. This is one of the best-established results in human population genetics: **"Evidence for balancing selection at HLA,"** PubMed 6884768 (Hedrick & Thomson-era classical result), and confirmed with modern methods in **"Joint estimation of selection intensity and mutation rate under balancing selection with applications to HLA,"** *Genetics* 2022, https://academic.oup.com/genetics/article/221/2/iyac058/6569836 (bioRxiv: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.11.18.469194v1.full).
- The classical test is the **Ewens–Watterson homozygosity test**: Watterson extended Ewens' neutral sampling formula to the case of symmetric overdominance (s>0), and applying it to real HLA-A/B data across 22 populations rejected neutrality in **25/44 population-locus combinations** (significantly *less* homozygosity than the neutral Ewens expectation) — i.e., HLA loci are close to the textbook example of a locus where the infinite-alleles neutral model demonstrably fails.
- **Practical consequence for your saturation estimate**: because balancing selection keeps rare/novel alleles at higher-than-neutral frequency and diversifies the allele pool faster than drift-mutation alone would, a **neutral-model-based estimator (Ewens/Watterson θ) will be systematically biased** — likely **underestimating** the number of undiscovered alleles, since a purely neutral model expects allele-frequency spectra to be more L-shaped (many rare singletons, few common alleles) than balancing selection actually produces, and a badly-fit neutral model could also miscalibrate the "how many more individuals until saturation" prediction in either direction depending on which part of the spectrum you weight most.
- **Why Chao-family estimators are comparatively more robust here**: Chao1/Chao2 do **not** assume a specific underlying allele-frequency distribution (no infinite-alleles/neutral assumption) — they are nonparametric lower-bound estimators that only use the counts of rare classes (singletons/doubletons or uniques/duplicates) and a general inequality argument (Chao 1984, formalized further in Chao & Colwell's later Hill-number/iNEXT work). This is precisely why the ecology literature moved to Chao-style estimators over parametric species-abundance-curve fits for exactly this kind of selection/detectability-heterogeneity concern. **This is the primary justification for recommending Chao2 over an Ewens/Watterson-θ-based approach** in §3.4.
- **Residual risk even with Chao2**: Chao estimators still assume that **each existing allele has some homogeneous, sample-independent probability of being observed** (or is at least only moderately heterogeneous — ACE/ICE partially correct for this). Balancing selection does **not** violate this assumption in the same direct way it violates the infinite-alleles model — it changes the frequency *distribution* of alleles, not the fundamental logic of "singletons and doubletons carry information about the unseen tail" — so Chao-style estimators remain valid, but you should still: (a) report bootstrap CIs, not point estimates alone, since selection-driven distributional differences will widen them appropriately if your data behaves less like the classical iid case; (b) run ACE alongside Chao2 as a robustness check, since ACE is specifically designed for the case where rare-class detection probability is uneven; (c) explicitly flag in the write-up that **no existing richness estimator was designed with balancing selection in mind**, and that your Chao2 numbers should be read as the best available nonparametric lower bound, not a mechanistic population-genetic model fit.
- **Do not use Watterson's θ or a raw Ewens-formula-based estimate of undiscovered allele count as your headline number.** Use it only as a secondary, explicitly-labeled "neutral-model-would-predict..." comparison point to illustrate, quantitatively, how much the selection-driven excess diversity matters — i.e., turn the neutrality violation into a *result* (a demonstration that HLA discovery curves are structured differently than a neutral locus would look) rather than trying to route around it.

## 5. Practical pitfalls specific to assembly-based calling in the MHC

- **DRB paralog copy-number variation**: HLA-DRB3/DRB4/DRB5 are presence/absence paralogs of DRB1 — haplotypes carry DRB1 plus exactly one of DRB3, DRB4, or DRB5 (or none), so gene content itself varies by haplotype, and misassembly here is well documented: **"Improved haplotype resolution of highly duplicated MHC genes in a long-read genome assembly using MiSeq amplicons,"** PMC10349553, notes that some MHC-IIB scaffolds carrying 6–19 paralogs were **incorrectly split across "different loci" rather than correctly resolved as alternate haplotypes of the same locus** in prior assemblies — i.e., paralog miscounting/misassignment is a known, recurring class of error in this exact gene family. Typing/copy-number method precedent: Zhang et al., "Typing and copy number determination for HLA-DRB3, -DRB4 and -DRB5 from next-generation sequencing data," *HLA* 2017, PubMed 28145101 (k-means clustering on DRB3/4/5:DRB1 read-depth ratio).
- **Gene conversion**: local sequence homology between DRB paralogs and pseudogenes suggests a history of **non-homologous recombination/gene conversion**, meaning a "novel" allele call in DRB genes specifically deserves extra scrutiny for chimeric assembly vs. genuine gene-conversion-derived diversity (PMC3494273, "Evolution of HLA-DRB Genes").
- **Reference bias in the assembly itself**, not just in IPD-IMGT/HLA: general MHC-assembly review, **"State-of-the-art genome inference in the human MHC,"** ScienceDirect S1357272520301990, covers assembly-graph and reference-bias pitfalls broadly across MHC typing/assembly methods.
- **Concrete sanity checks to build into your pipeline** (synthesizing all of the above and §1.4):
  1. Per-haplotype independence check — a novel call should be internally consistent with a single, non-chimeric local assembly graph structure at that locus (flag calls at contig breakpoints/low-coverage junctions if such QC metadata is available from your assembly pipeline).
  2. Recurrence across **unrelated** individuals as the strongest single piece of evidence for genuine biology over one-off artifact (§1.4 item 5).
  3. Ancestry clustering of a novel allele's carriers — a genuinely old, population-restricted allele should cluster within one or a few ancestry groups/haplogroups rather than appearing at random across every ancestry (random scatter is more consistent with recurrent independent assembly error than with real allelic identity by descent).
  4. Synonymous:non-synonymous ratio of novel-defining substitutions as an aggregate sanity check (as in Zhou et al.'s substitution-rate argument, §0) — a novel set dominated by high-impact frameshift/stop-codon changes with no plausible pseudogene status is more likely artifact-heavy than one dominated by missense/synonymous SNPs matching the expected coding-sequence mutation spectrum.
  5. DRB-specific extra gate: require correct paralog copy-number resolution (via read-depth ratio per Zhang et al. method, if depth data is available) before trusting any DRB3/4/5 novel call.

## 6. Actionable ordered workflow (summary)

1. **Definition**: a novel allele candidate = Immuannot `new` tag / `template_distance > 0` on a full-length CDS with **no** `template_warning`.
2. **QC filter**: drop homopolymer-indel-only calls (Huijse et al. filter, §1.3); require CDS completeness; annotate synonymous/non-synonymous and PBR-exon location (leveraging Immuannot's field-position `new`-tag placement, §2.1).
3. **Cluster/collapse**: group novel calls by exact CDS/ARD sequence identity into distinct putative-allele clusters (pre-G-group logic, §2.2–2.3); assign provisional IDs `<gene>*<nearest-known-allele>_novelNN`.
4. **Confidence tiering**: singleton (1 haplotype, 1 individual) vs. recurrent (≥2 unrelated individuals) vs. cross-ancestry-recurrent; compute substitution-rate/indel-ratio sanity check per Zhou et al. (§0, §1.4 item 7) on the whole novel set as a global QC gate.
5. **Per-ancestry counting**: tabulate distinct collapsed novel alleles (and total alleles = known+novel) per gene per AoU ancestry group (AFR/AMR/EAS/EUR/MID/SAS), using haplotypes as the sampling unit.
6. **Saturation estimation**: run Chao2 (incidence-based) + ACE, per gene per ancestry group, plus iNEXT-style rarefaction/extrapolation curves with bootstrap CIs (§3.4); report "% of estimated true richness observed" = S_obs / (S_obs + Chao2 unseen estimate) as your headline "% discovered" statistic, directly analogous to the Abi-Rached et al. 78%-of-expected-diversity framing (§3.1) but computed with a stated, citable nonparametric method (an improvement on that precedent, where the estimator identity was not confirmed in this pass — recommend explicitly fetching and reading the Abi-Rached primary text before writing your own methods section, to either replicate or explicitly differentiate your estimator choice).
7. **Neutrality-violation framing**: report a secondary neutral-model (Ewens/Watterson-θ-implied) prediction of undiscovered alleles alongside the Chao2 number specifically to *quantify* the balancing-selection effect (§4) — turn the assumption violation into a result rather than a caveat buried in methods.
8. **Validation layer**: apply the assembly-specific sanity checks in §5 (paralog copy number, gene-conversion flags, ancestry-clustering-of-carriers) as a final gate before any claim of "novel allele" in the paper's headline numbers; reserve full IPD-IMGT/HLA submission (full-genomic-sequence standard, §1.1) for the highest-confidence, recurrent, cross-validated subset.
