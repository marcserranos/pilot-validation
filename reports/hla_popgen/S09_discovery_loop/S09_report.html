At a glance

What this sprint asked: do HLA, KIR and complement C4 variants resolved by long-read sequencing explain anything in blood RNA, health records or plasma proteins that short-read data could not? Cohort: up to 12,233 All of Us long-read genomes, blood RNA-seq (3,141–8,000 people depending on the test), Olink plasma proteomics (up to 8,884) and health records.

Most important or most novel findings

  1. HLA-E R107G lowers the NKG2A (KLRC1) RNA in blood, about 6% per copy. Lead Protein not yet tested The best new mechanistic lead. It is specific (the 7 other NK and immune genes tested do not move), has the same sign in all five ancestries, and survives ancestry and local-genetics controls (z ≈ 5, p ≈ 7e-7). It is modest in size (6%) and about 20 hypotheses were tried in this sprint without correction for that. The plasma protein test is null, but plasma only holds the soluble fraction, so the protein-level question is open. See section 4.
  2. One HLA allele, HLA-B*14:02, moves three unrelated plasma proteins by 1.4–2.6 SD. Open question Partly known The biggest effect we saw (about 470 carriers among 8,884). It is allele-specific and the same size in AFR, AMR and EUR. UK Biobank already reported that HLA explains these proteins but named no allele. The cause is unresolved and an assay or plasma artefact is a serious candidate: blood RNA is flat, but these proteins are probably made mostly outside blood cells, so that says little. Treat it as a lead and a warning about Olink HLA studies until a second assay is run. See section 7.
  3. The long-read KIR and C4 calls are independently validated by plasma protein levels. Validation KIR3DL1 gene content predicts the KIR3DL1 protein with z = 51 and C4B copy number predicts C4B protein (z = 29), in all five ancestries. This is a method-validation result, useful for the paper. See section 6.
  4. Native short-read HLA calls are accurate at the resolution that matters. Validation DQA1 looks 52% concordant by allele name but 99% by peptide-binding groove (G group). This closed off a tempting idea ("native errors distort disease studies"). See section 1.
  5. Five real HLA-F loss-of-function alleles; the HLA-E and HLA-G ones were artefacts. Strong Raw long reads support 18 of 26 recurrent unnamed variants in every examined carrier (up to 8 per variant), including HLA-F T237I and P205T. Each loss-of-function allele is in fewer than 20 people. Whether they are new to IPD is the subject of the S08 report. See section 3.

Most promising lines of research (in order)

  1. Test HLA-E R107G → NKG2A at the protein level. Flow cytometry for surface NKG2A on NK cells from donors chosen by HLA-E genotype. It is the only decisive test left and the effect is specific enough to justify it. Needs a wet-lab partner or a recontact cohort; cannot be done in the Workbench.
  2. Settle HLA-B*14:02 → LRPAP1 / LRP1 / SGSH. Measure LRPAP1 by an independent method (ELISA or mass spectrometry) in carriers, check the UK Biobank supplement and liver eQTLs. If real it explains the "unclear and intriguing" HLA–LRPAP1 link that the UK Biobank authors left open; if an assay artefact it is a useful warning for every Olink HLA study.
  3. Write up the HLA/KIR/C4 → proteome atlas. Five ancestries, long-read-resolved alleles, KIR content and C4 copy number on a 5,415-protein panel. Only the top 60 of 780,000 associations have been looked at; mining the rest (for example which KIR haplotypes move soluble immune proteins) is cheap.
  4. Prepare the IPD submission for HLA-F T237I and P205T (see the S08 report). The raw-read pile-up here already supports both in every examined carrier (up to 8; relatedness among them was not re-checked); what is still missing is an independent trio and phased full-length exons.
  5. Replicate C4A copy number → arterial disorders (I77), only if a second cohort with C4 copy number exists. A lead, not a finding. After adjusting for ancestry PCs and healthcare use the combined z is 4.14 against a 4.07 threshold (3.91 in one version of the adjustment), the signal is almost entirely European, and it rests on one diagnosis code.

Underwhelming or negative (still informative)

  • No disease-association discovery, and the scan was underpowered. It produced no family-wise HLA hit, but it could not even confirm its own best positive control (type 1 diabetes reaches z = 5.5, below the 6.98 threshold). So "no hits" means "too little power", not "HLA does not matter here"; the native 535k cohort would be needed. Its strongest signal (DQA1*05:03 → liver/sepsis codes) was ancestry confounding inside one group and is retired. Retired
  • KIR gene content does not change NK-cell RNA (n=5,120) and C4 copy number does not tune interferon: clean nulls with working controls, ruling out effects larger than about 0.01 SD per KIR gene. Null result
  • B*27 does not trigger an unfolded-protein-response signature in blood (n=5,642). Null result
  • Class II expression differences inside identical-groove alleles are tiny (0.2–0.6% of variance) and mapping bias is not excluded. Weak signal
  • Plasma NKG2A is null (n=2,709), but plasma holds only the soluble fraction, so it is inconclusive rather than a refutation.
  • The think tank's "naive looks" (quick histograms and tables) were proposed but never run. Four of them are implemented in a script that has only been tested on synthetic data, because the VM was unreachable when this report was rebuilt. See section 8.
Cheat-sheet How to read the numbers in this report (30 seconds)
z-score
How many "standard errors" an effect is from zero. About |z| > 2 is conventionally "interesting" for one test; with thousands of tests we need |z| ≳ 6 (see the threshold printed in each section). |z| of 50 is absurdly strong.
p-value
Chance of a result at least this extreme if there were no effect. 3e-8 means 3 in 100 million.
SD
Standard deviation, the typical person-to-person spread. "+2.6 SD" means carriers sit about two and a half spreads above everyone else.
log2 TPM
RNA amount on a log2 scale. −0.09 ≈ 6% less RNA; −1 = half.
NPX
The Olink plasma-protein unit, also log2: +1 NPX = twice the signal.
λ (lambda)
Calibration check for a scan of many tests: 1.0 means the test statistics behave as chance predicts; well above 1 means something (usually ancestry) inflates them.
Family-wise threshold
The z a result must exceed so that fewer than 1 in 20 scans of pure noise would show any hit. We estimate it by shuffling labels (permutation).
Pooled vs meta
Pooled: everyone analysed together with ancestry adjusted. Meta: each ancestry analysed separately, then the estimates combined (so one group cannot dominate).
Dosage
Copies of an allele or gene a person carries (0, 1 or 2).
Control
A test whose answer we know (positive = must be non-zero, negative = must be zero). A result is only believed if the controls behave.
Ancestry / PCs
People are grouped by genetic ancestry (AFR, AMR, EAS, EUR, SAS, MID). "PCs" are numbers summarising finer ancestry within a group; adding them removes ancestry confounding.
Replicates in 5/5 ancestries
The effect has the same sign in each ancestry separately, so it is unlikely to be one group's quirk.

1. Are the native short-read HLA calls wrong? Idea killed

Bottom lineNo, not where it matters. The All of Us native calls agree with long-read truth for 98–99% of genotypes when judged at the level of the peptide-binding groove. The apparent errors at the allele-name level (DQA1 only 52%) are a resolution effect: short reads cannot tell apart alleles that differ outside the groove. The hypothesis that native errors distort HLA–disease studies was dropped.
0.52 → 0.99DQA1 agreement, allele name vs groove (G group)
12,208people with both native and long-read calls
6 genesHLA-A, C, DPB1, DQA1, DQB1, DRB1
Background What 'resolution' and 'G group' mean

An allele name has up to four colon-separated fields; only the first two change the protein. A G group collects alleles that are identical over exons 2 and 3, the peptide-binding groove that T cells and NK cells read. A caller that sees only those exons can be right about the groove and unable to name the allele.

How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

For each person and gene, the genotype from the AoU native short-read callers was compared with the long-read assembly call (Immuannot, template distance 0 = ground-truth-like). Names were compared as 2-field strings; groove-level comparison maps each allele to its G group using IPD-IMGT/HLA hla_nom_g.txt.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • G-group concordance is an upper bound: it ignores differences outside exons 2 and 3.
  • Long-read calls are the reference here but are not perfect; the 'template distance 0' subset is the most trustworthy.
  • HLA-B is not in the comparison table and the run notes do not record why.
  • This closes the 'native errors' idea for peptide-binding questions; it does not say native calls capture structural variation or phase, which long reads add.

2. Do KIR content, C4 copies and allele families change blood RNA? Mostly null

Bottom lineThe controls work and the questions come back empty. Having a KIR gene, or more C4A/C4B copies, raises that gene's own RNA as it should. But KIR gene content does not shift NK-cell RNA (n=5,120) and C4 copy number does not shift interferon-response RNA. A small extra effect of non-groove variation on class II expression exists (0.2–0.6% of variance) but mapping bias is not excluded.

Background Why a positive control matters here

Short-read RNA maps poorly across KIR genes, which are near-identical. Before believing 'KIR content does not change NK RNA' we first check that KIR2DL1 and KIR2DL3 copy number does change KIR2DL1 and KIR2DL3 RNA. It does (z ≈ 13–28). KIR3DL1 and KIR2DS4 come out reversed (a mapping artefact between loci), so gene-level KIR RNA is only trusted for some genes.

How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 101 streams the RNA-SeQC2 TPM file for the blood RNA-seq cohort and keeps a panel of KIR, HLA, complement, lineage-marker and interferon genes. Script 102 regresses log2(TPM+1) on the long-read feature with ancestry dummies (groups under 20 dropped); NK and interferon scores are the mean of z-scored marker genes. Script 103 compares nested models (G group vs G group + 3-field allele) among homozygotes and builds a permutation null.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • RNA is aligned to GRCh38, so haplotypes far from the reference can look lower for mapping reasons. Next step: re-quantify on personal two-haplotype references (S05/S08 machinery).
  • Bulk blood RNA mixes cell types; NK scores are composition proxies.
  • Nulls here exclude effects larger than roughly the confidence intervals shown, not tiny ones.

3. HLA-E, F and G: which unnamed variants are real? Strong

Bottom lineHLA-F, not HLA-E or HLA-G, is where real protein-truncating variation sits. Raw long reads support 18 of 26 recurrent unnamed signatures in every carrier and in none of 8 controls, including five HLA-F loss-of-function alleles (stop-gains E70*, Q75*, W265*, R294*; start-loss M1V; each in fewer than 20 unrelated people, with one RNA carrier each). Up to 8 carriers per signature were examined. The HLA-E and HLA-G stop-gain calls and a DPA1 call had no read support: they were assembly artefacts. Whether any of these alleles is new to IPD is the subject of the S08 report.
18 / 26unnamed signatures supported by raw reads in every carrier
0 / 8matched control people with the variant
26%of HLA-F RNA reads from the stop-gain copy (R294*)
4–5×more HLA-G 'null or questionable-suffix' calls in AFR than SAS/EUR (a lead)
Background raw reads, assemblies and nonsense-mediated decay

Long reads are stitched into an assembly, and alleles are called from the assembly. If the assembly is wrong at one base, a 'novel allele' appears that no read supports, so we return to the raw reads. A premature stop signal makes the cell destroy the message (nonsense-mediated decay); the carrier then shows fewer RNA reads from the mutated copy.

How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 105: GENCODE v46 CDS → GRCh38 coordinates (strand-aware, verified against assembly bases), samtools pileup on raw AoU long-read BAMs of carriers and matched controls. Script 96: RNA reads re-aligned to a personal two-haplotype transcript reference built from the carrier's own assembly, with a simulated-read bias control. Script 106: allele frequencies and heterozygosity from 400 unrelated people per ancestry.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • Three 'supported' signatures are uninformative because the alternate base is present in every control.
  • 'Supported' means real DNA variation, not 'new': names are checked against IPD (see the S08 report).
  • Loss-of-function frequencies per allele are below 20 people, so no counts are shown.

4. HLA-E R107G and NKG2A: the best mechanistic lead Strong at RNAProtein untested

Bottom linePeople who carry the stable HLA-E variant 107G make about 6% less NKG2A (KLRC1) RNA per copy. The effect is specific to KLRC1, has the same sign in all five ancestries, is independent of NK-cell abundance and interferon tone, and survives ancestry (ten PCs) and local-genetics (three KLRC1-region variants) controls. The only protein-level test available (plasma) is null, but plasma is the soluble fraction. Surface NKG2A on NK cells has not been measured.
−0.09log2 TPM per 107G copy (≈ 6% less KLRC1 RNA)
3.6e-8 → 7e-7meta p-value, before and after the extra controls
1 of 8other NK/immune genes that move (KLRC1; the 7 others are flat)
n = 3,141people with blood RNA and all class I calls (2,975 in the 5-ancestry meta; the other 166 are in groups too small to analyse alone, mainly MID)
Background what HLA-E, NKG2A and 107G are

NK cells kill stressed cells but spare healthy ones. They check HLA class I on the target through HLA-E, which displays a fragment of other class I molecules' leader sequence; the NK receptor CD94/NKG2A (genes KLRD1/KLRC1) reads it and sends a brake signal. Everyone carries R107 (E*01:01) or G107 (E*01:03, more stable at the cell surface). We asked whether the version you carry changes how much NKG2A your NK cells make.

Background why ancestry PCs and a cis control

A skeptic can say: people with 107G may also tend to carry a KLRC1-region variant that lowers KLRC1. Two different chromosomes are only correlated through ancestry, so we test it directly: add ten ancestry PCs, then the strongest local variants (extracted from short-read genotypes; the lead variant alone has p = 3.7e-143, so the control is powerful). The measured correlation between 107G and those variants is ≤ 0.05.

Evidence A failed mechanism for contrast: HLA-B*27 and the unfolded-protein response

The 1999 misfolding hypothesis says B*27 misfolds and triggers an ER stress response that fuels spondyloarthritis. In blood RNA of 5,642 people, B*27 dose does not change a UPR module score (β = −0.016 ± 0.024, p = 0.49); ERAP1/2, IL23A, BiP and XBP1 are individually null, and B*07, B*57, housekeeping and T-cell controls are flat. A well-powered human null in whole blood (not in the affected joint tissue).

Evidence More detail on the HLA-E effect
  • Dose pattern: heterozygotes −0.144, homozygotes −0.159 (near-dominant).
  • Not mediated by HLA-E RNA (adding HLA-E TPM as a covariate leaves the estimate at −0.091).
  • Leader-peptide hypothesis (HLA class I signal-peptide methionine at −21 supplying HLA-E): only nominal (meta p ≈ 0.02), attenuated by NK abundance.
  • Closest prior work: Le Luduec et al. 2024, about 100–200 donors, found no HLA-E allele effect on the frequency of NKG2A-positive NK cells (a different readout and a much smaller sample). We found no report of the RNA effect; the literature search was not exhaustive.
  • Strength: z ≈ 5.5 before and ≈ 5.0 after the extra controls, from a test whose outcome gene (KLRC1) followed from the biology, but about 20 hypotheses were tried across this sprint and no correction for that is applied.
How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 108 regresses log2(TPM+1) of KLRC1 and control genes on E*01:03 dose with age, age², sex, ancestry dummies and a composition block (T/B/myeloid markers); the fully adjusted model adds NK abundance, interferon score, CD8/γδ T content, KLRC2 RNA and the HLA leader-peptide load. Per-ancestry results are combined by inverse-variance meta-analysis. S5 adds ten ancestry PCs (All of Us ancestry_preds). S6 adds the strongest independent KLRC1-region variants (1,756 variants from chr12:10.2–10.7 Mb in plink2). Script 111 repeats the test on Olink plasma proteins.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong, and what would change our mind
  • Not excluded: CMV serostatus (adaptive NK cells; KLRC2 RNA is only a partial proxy), a KLRC2 gene deletion (only controlled through KLRC2 RNA and the nearby variants), other chr6 variants in linkage with E*01:03, bulk-RNA cell-type attribution.
  • Protein level unknown. A flow-cytometry result showing no difference in surface NKG2A per NK cell would make this a transcript-only curiosity.
  • The effect is small (6%). It matters mainly as a specific, replicated, mechanistic signal.

Bottom lineThe scan found no disease discovery. Background PheWAS, calibration and why strong hits are often false

A phenome-wide scan tests every allele against hundreds of diagnoses. With ~100,000 tests some hits are chance, so we use negative-control outcomes (injury, external-cause and symptom chapters that should not depend on HLA), label permutations for a family-wise threshold, and the inflation factor λ (1.0 is ideal) as a calibration check. The biggest trap is confounding: within a broad group like AMR people differ in ancestral mix, which affects both which alleles they carry and which diagnoses they get; ancestry PCs are the standard fix.

Background Reading key for this section
  • λ: calibration; 1.0 is ideal.
  • Family-wise threshold: the |z| a hit must pass so that noise alone would rarely produce any hit across all ~7,000 tests (from 20 label shuffles).
  • Negative-control chapters: diagnosis chapters (injury, external causes, symptoms) that should not depend on HLA; they set the baseline for false hits.

How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 107: logistic score tests (ancestry, sex, age and age² as covariates) for each allele × 3-character ICD-10 category in unrelated long-read people; BH false-discovery control; family-wise threshold from 20 label permutations within ancestry×sex strata. The first version was discarded: negative-control chapters showed 269 false hits because near-constant common alleles broke the score-test arithmetic. Fixing it (centred exposures, minimum variance, conditioned permutations, regression test) restored calibration. Lesson: a miscalibrated negative control is a stop sign; do not read the top hits. Script 109 re-tests leads within each ancestry with a healthcare-utilisation covariate and ten ancestry PCs.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • Binary diagnoses have little power below ~100 cases (the multiple sclerosis and psoriasis controls are underpowered).
  • Only the top hits per exposure set were followed up.
  • Native 535k cohort not used; it would give the power this scan lacks.

Background how Olink measures a protein, what NPX and a pQTL are

Olink Explore HT measures ~5,400 plasma proteins with a proximity extension assay: two antibodies, each with a DNA tag, must bind the same protein before the tags pair and are counted. The result is NPX (log2 scale). A gene variant that shifts a protein's level is a pQTL. Plasma holds the soluble fraction, and because the readout depends on antibodies a variant can change the signal without changing the amount (an epitope effect).

How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 112 builds a person × assay matrix (54 million Olink records, passing sample and assay QC, replicates averaged; assays with <70% coverage dropped), regresses every protein on every exposure (144: HLA alleles with ≥40 carriers, C4A/C4B copy number, KIR gene content) with age, age², sex, ancestry dummies and ten ancestry PCs by residual-projection OLS, and sets the family-wise threshold (|z| ≥ 5.91) by 10 permutations within ancestry×sex strata. Top hits are re-estimated within each ancestry.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • Only the top 60 of 780,000 associations have been examined.
  • Plasma = soluble fraction, and antibodies can be sensitive to amino-acid changes: strong HLA→HLA-protein hits are probably epitope effects.
  • Cohort enrichment: 8,884 of the 9,969 Olink participants are in the long-read cohort, so power is high but the cohort is not population-representative.

7. HLA-B*14:02 and three unrelated plasma proteins Open questionPartly known

Bottom lineOne HLA allele shifts three proteins encoded on three other chromosomes, but the cause is unknown. HLA-B*14:02 carriers (about 470 people) sit about +2.6 SD for LRPAP1, +1.7 SD for LRP1 and +1.4 SD for SGSH. The effect is allele-specific and the same size in AFR, AMR and EUR. Of 5,415 proteins only 8 pass the threshold: four are encoded in the MHC region (MICA/MICB, C4B, HSPA1A, CDSN) and four elsewhere (LRPAP1, LRP1, SGSH and, more weakly, GABARAP). Blood RNA for LRPAP1, LRP1 and SGSH is flat, but these proteins are probably made mostly outside blood cells, so that is not evidence against a real effect. An assay or plasma artefact is a serious candidate. The UK Biobank paper (Europeans) reports HLA explaining ~43% of LRPAP1, ~29% of SGSH and ~25% of LRP1 variance and calls it 'unclear and intriguing' without naming an allele; we attribute it to B*14:02 and show it replicates outside Europe.

Background haplotypes, trans effects and what the proteins are

HLA-B and HLA-C sit side by side on chromosome 6 and are inherited as a block (a haplotype): B*14:02 almost always comes with C*08:02, so either looks causal unless you model them together. The three affected proteins come from other chromosomes (4, 12, 17), so this is a trans effect. LRPAP1 is a chaperone of the LRP1 receptor family; SGSH is a lysosomal enzyme; they share no known HLA connection.

  • Co-variation: residuals of LRPAP1/LRP1/SGSH correlate in carriers (0.61/0.46/0.24 for LRPAP1~LRP1, LRPAP1~SGSH, LRP1~SGSH) and in non-carriers (0.40/0.32/0.15): the three proteins move together in everyone, suggesting a shared factor.
  • Forward selection among 237 HLA alleles for LRPAP1: B*14:02 (z 79) → C*08:02 (z 25.5) → C*07:18 (−3.8) → DRB1*01:02 (3.5).
  • Also past the threshold: MICA/MICB (−17), C4B (+10), HSPA1A (−7), CDSN (−6), all encoded in the MHC (expected, haplotype-linked), and GABARAP (+7), encoded on chromosome 17, which is a fourth non-MHC protein.
How it was done Scripts, models and thresholds

Script 113 reuses the cached person × assay matrix of script 112. It models LRPAP1/LRP1/SGSH on B*14:02, C*08:02, DRB1*01:02 and DQB1*05:01 singly and jointly (ancestry, sex, age and ten PCs), forward-selects among all alleles, scans per-residue dosages built from IPD-IMGT/HLA protein alignments for HLA-B and HLA-C before and after conditioning on B*14:02, and lists the B*14:02 z-score for every assay. Caveats and red-team What it could be, and what would settle it
  • Assay interference or antibody cross-reactivity: a plasma factor specific to B*14:02 carriers (for example soluble HLA-B*14:02) could change the readout of several assays at once. The proteins also co-vary in non-carriers, which fits a shared technical factor too.
  • A real effect outside cells: the haplotype could change how these proteins are secreted, cleared or shed (LRP1 is an endocytic receptor); blood RNA cannot see that.
  • Decisive: measure LRPAP1 by ELISA or mass spectrometry in carriers; check the UK Biobank supplement (does it name B*14:02?); look for B*14:02-haplotype effects on LRPAP1 in liver or other tissue eQTL resources.
Evidence Literature

Krishna et al., Nature Communications 2024 (UK Biobank, 45,330 Europeans, 2,940 Olink proteins): 504 proteins show HLA effects, >80% mapping to peptide-binding-groove positions; LRPAP1 (42.9% of variance explained by HLA), SGSH (29.2%) and LRP1 (24.9%) are among the top; no driving allele or residue is given for these in the text, and the authors note SGSH has no known immune role. Our result differs in attributing it to B*14:02 (with C*08:02 as tag) and in showing it outside Europeans. No assay-artefact analysis is reported there.

How it was done What the 'naive looks' would be and how they would be made

The think tank proposed ten quick tables and plots; the five below are the ones written down in the portfolio.

  1. Template-distance histograms (how far each person's HLA haplotypes are from the reference) by platform and ancestry: one column of the cohort membership table; a platform effect would show as shifted peaks.
  2. KIR gene-count per person by ancestry and platform: a missing-gene artefact would show as a low-count tail only on one platform.
  3. DRB1 vs DRB3/4/5 consistency: DRB1*03 should come with DRB3, DRB1*04 with DRB4, DRB1*15 with DRB5; exceptions are either real structural variants or calling gaps.
  4. Genes called per person by sequencing platform and trim tier.
  5. Haplotype block lengths between phase breaks, by ancestry.

Panels 1–4 are implemented in 115_naive_looks.py (written and tested on synthetic data; not yet run on the real data). Block lengths are not in the call tables. All outputs are aggregate histograms with counts under 20 suppressed, and take well under an hour of VM time.

Methods, data and reproduction

How it was done Data used
  • Long-read genomes: up to 12,233 All of Us participants (PacBio Revio and Sequel II/IIe, ONT); HLA and KIR alleles called from assemblies with Immuannot against IPD-IMGT/HLA 3.55 (re-checked against 3.66 in S08).
  • Native HLA calls: AoU short-read callers, 535k participants (12,208 overlap with long reads).
  • Blood RNA-seq: RNA-SeQC2 gene TPM (3,141 people for the HLA-E tests; 8,000 for the B*14:02 RNA test).
  • Plasma proteomics: Olink Explore HT, 9,969 participants, 5,415 assays; 8,884 unrelated long-read people used.
  • Health records: OMOP ICD-10 3-character categories with at least two visits.
  • Ancestry: AoU predicted ancestry and its first ten PCs.

Background The loop used to generate and filter ideas

Haiku think-tank personas (moonshot, secure claim, naive, heuristic) proposed ideas, a stronger model with web search critiqued them, each surviving idea got a ledger row with a kill criterion before it ran, and every result passed a red-team checklist (positive and negative controls, chance baseline, relatedness, ancestry, platform, read support, independent truth, suppression).
