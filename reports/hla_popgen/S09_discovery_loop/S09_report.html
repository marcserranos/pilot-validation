1. The loop

2. Native short-read calls are right where it matters, and blind where it doesn't

Background: what is an HLA allele name, and what does 'resolution' mean?

HLA genes (HLA-A, -B, -C, DRB1, …) are the most variable genes in humans. Each version is an allele, named with up to four colon-separated fields. The first two fields change the protein; the last two usually do not. At the usual 2-field level a typing tool has to be right about every amino-acid-changing difference.

What the immune system cares about is the peptide-binding groove, built from exons 2 and 3. A G group is a set of alleles that are identical over exactly those exons. Exon-based typing tools (like the short-read callers in All of Us) can only be judged fairly at G-group level, which is why the 'error' we first saw (DQA1 0.52) vanished at G-group level (0.99).

5. Second wave: raw reads, landscape, mechanisms

5a. Raw long-read evidence for the unnamed alleles

Background: raw reads, assembly artefacts, stop-gains and nonsense-mediated decay

A sequencing run produces millions of reads; software stitches them into an assembly and calls alleles from it. If the assembly is wrong at one position, a 'novel allele' can appear that no read supports. So we go back to the raw reads: a real inherited variant is present in every read at that position in carriers and in none in non-carriers.

A stop-gain puts an early STOP signal in the gene. The cell often destroys such messages (nonsense-mediated decay), so the mutated copy contributes fewer RNA reads than the normal copy. Seeing only ~26% of RNA reads from the stop-gain copy is therefore evidence that the allele is truly loss-of-function.

For 26 candidate unnamed signatures we located the exact GRCh38 position from the GENCODE CDS model (checked against the assembly bases) and piled up the raw AoU long-read alignments, independent of the assembly the call came from, in up to 8 carriers and 8 matched non-carriers. 18 of 26 are supported in every examined carrier and in 0 of 8 controls: HLA-F T237I and P205T, T279I, G128R, G37S, V309I, A322T, M119I, Q247R, three recurrent synonymous signatures, and five HLA-F loss-of-function alleles (stop-gains E70*, Q75*, W265*, R294*; start-loss M1V; each in fewer than 20 unrelated people). The HLA-E (Y28*, G321*) and HLA-G (R181*, Y142*) stop-gain calls and DPA1 E210G have no raw-read support: assembly artefacts. Three “supported” signatures (HLA-C and HLA-G synonymous, DRB1 S29A) are uninformative because the alt base is in every control. So HLA-F, not E or G, is where real protein-truncating variation sits.

5b. Do the HLA-F loss-of-function alleles lower their own transcript?

One heterozygous carrier per allele on a personal two-haplotype reference: the stop-gain R294* haplotype carries only 26% of informative HLA-F reads (bias-corrected log2 −1.23, ≈2.3× lower; consistent with nonsense-mediated decay), the start-loss M1V 40% (transcript persists, translation lost), and the missense variants 35–50%. Single-carrier evidence only.

5c. HLA-E, F and G: diversity landscape by ancestry

At equal sample size (400 unrelated people per ancestry): HLA-E is balanced (expected heterozygosity 0.50–0.52 in all six ancestries — the stable R107G polymorphism); HLA-F is nearly monomorphic and ancestry-structured (top allele 96% in EAS, 80% in AFR); HLA-G carries 4–5× more null/questionable-suffix haplotypes in AFR (6.7%) than in SAS or EUR (1.4–1.6%). For scale, HLA-B has 56–86 distinct 2-field alleles.

5d. A mechanism that failed, and one that did not

Background: HLA-E, NKG2A and why 107G matters

NK cells kill infected or stressed cells but must spare healthy ones. They check for HLA class I on the target. HLA-E displays a fragment of the leader (signal) sequence of the other class I molecules; the NK receptor CD94/NKG2A (genes KLRD1/KLRC1) reads it and sends a brake signal: 'self is intact'.

Almost everyone carries one of two HLA-E versions, differing at amino acid 107: R107 (E*01:01) or G107 (E*01:03, the more stable one at the cell surface). We asked whether the version you carry changes how much NKG2A your blood's NK cells make. The TPM numbers in this report are RNA counts per million, shown on a log2 scale (−0.09 ≈ 6% less).

HLA-B*27 does not induce an unfolded-protein-response signature in blood RNA (UPR module β = −0.016 ± 0.024, p = 0.49, n = 5,642; ERAP1/2, IL23A, BiP, XBP1 all null; B*07, B*57, housekeeping and T-cell controls flat): a well-powered human test of the 1999 misfolding hypothesis in whole blood, informative as a null.

HLA-E R107G (E*01:03) lowers NKG2A (KLRC1) expression per NK cell. The effect is specific to KLRC1, independent of NK abundance, interferon tone, CD8/γδ T-cell content, KLRC2 (adaptive-NK proxy) and the HLA leader peptides, has the same sign in all five ancestries, is not mediated by HLA-E mRNA, and is near-dominant (heterozygote β −0.144, homozygote −0.159). No prior report was found (closest: ~100–200 donors, no effect on NKG2A frequency); novelty is unverified.

Now excluded: ancestry structure (10 PCs, pooled and per ancestry) and KLRC1-region cis variation (see 5f). Still open: CMV serostatus (adaptive NK; KLRC2 level only a partial proxy), other chr6 variants in linkage with E*01:03, bulk-RNA cell-type attribution, and any protein-level confirmation on the NK cell surface (plasma NKG2A, n = 2,709, is null but measures the soluble fraction).

5e. Hypothesis-free association scan

Background: what is a phenome-wide scan, and why do 'strong' hits so often turn out false?

A phenome-wide association scan (PheWAS) tests every allele against hundreds of diseases from health records. With ~100,000 tests, some hits are chance, so we compare against negative-control outcomes (diagnosis chapters that should not depend on HLA, such as injuries) and against label permutations. The genomic inflation λ summarises whether the whole test cloud is miscalibrated (1.0 is ideal).

The biggest trap is confounding: within a broad group like 'AMR' people differ in their ancestral mix, which affects both which alleles they carry and (through environment and access to care) which diagnoses they receive. Ancestry principal components (PCs) are numbers that summarise this fine ancestry; adding them is the standard fix.

The HLA / KIR / C4 x ICD-10 phenome scan (594 outcome categories with at least 30 cases in 11,856 unrelated long-read people; score tests with ancestry, sex and age; negative-control outcome chapters; 20-permutation max|z| thresholds) first failed its own calibration check (numerical problem with near-constant common alleles) and was rerun. Corrected calibration is acceptable for HLA (lambda 1.12; negative chapters 1.13) and C4, but not for KIR (negative chapters lambda 1.79), so KIR hits are treated as ancestry-linked gene-content structure until shown otherwise. No HLA allele passes the family-wise threshold (max|z| 6.98). Known biology is recovered at the nominal level for type 1 diabetes (DQB1*02:01 -> E10, z 5.5; DQA1*05:01/*03:03 -> E10). The B*27:05 -> iridocyclitis hit (H20, z 4.7) looked like the classical B*27 anterior-uveitis association but is not a clean recovery: EUR (125 carriers) has no carrier cases, the largest z comes from a sparse SAS stratum (21 carriers), and AFR/AMR are positive (z 2.3 / 4.1 with PCs) on under 20 carrier cases each, so it stays inconclusive. The strongest HLA signal, DQA1*05:03 -> E87/K72/K70/A41 (z 5.6-5.9), hits four unrelated outcomes that are all severe-liver/critical-illness phenotypes. Within ancestry it is carried by AMR alone (234 of the 304 carriers; E87 z 6.45 in AMR, unchanged by healthcare-utilisation adjustment) and it collapses once ten ancestry PCs are added within AMR (E87 z 2.0; K72 1.0, K70 1.3, A41 1.8; meta z 1.8): DQA1*05:03 behaves as a marker of ancestry structure inside the AMR group (most likely admixture proportion, which can track liver-disease and critical-illness risk), not an HLA effect, and is retired. C4A copy number -> I77 (other arterial disorders) is concordant in sign in all six ancestries and survives PC adjustment (EUR z 4.32 with PCs; PC- and utilisation-adjusted meta z 4.14 against the 4.07 threshold, negative-control chapters max|z| 2.5), but it rests on EUR (leave-EUR-out meta z 1.3) and a single outcome code, so it is a lead requiring replication (C4 copy number from short reads in the native cohort). Odds ratios in sparse cells (tens to hundreds) are score-test artefacts and are not interpreted. Honest summary: the scan validates the pipeline and yields leads, not a discovery.

5f. Does the HLA-E → NKG2A effect survive ancestry and local genetics?

Background: ancestry principal components and 'cis' controls

A genetic effect on a gene's activity is called cis when the controlling variant sits next to the gene (here, variants within a few hundred thousand letters of KLRC1) and trans when it acts from far away (HLA-E is on a different chromosome). Cis variants usually have big, easy-to-detect effects.

A skeptic's question: maybe people who carry HLA-E 107G also tend to carry a particular KLRC1-region variant, and that lowers KLRC1. Two chromosomes are only associated through shared ancestry, so we test it directly: add ten ancestry PCs, then add the strongest cis variants, and see whether the HLA-E effect survives.

Two controls were added after the first pass. Ancestry structure: ten ancestry PCs in the full model, pooled and within each ancestry (the control that retired the DQA1*05:03 hits above). Cis variation at KLRC1: we extracted 1,756 variants at chr12:10.2–10.7 Mb from the short-read genotypes of the same 3,140 people; the region holds a very strong KLRC1 cis-eQTL (lead variant p = 3.7e-143), so the control is well powered. E*01:03 dose is essentially uncorrelated with the three independent lead variants (|r| ≤ 0.05). The association shrinks by about 17% and stays highly significant.

Protein level. All of Us has Olink Explore HT plasma proteomics (5,415 assays). In 2,709 people plasma KLRC1 does not move with 107G (+0.023 NPX, p = 0.59; meta p = 0.38). Plasma KLRC1 is the shed/soluble fraction, so this neither confirms nor refutes the RNA result; surface NKG2A by flow cytometry in genotype-selected donors is the decisive experiment. 107G is nominally positive for KLRK1 (meta p = 0.005), KLRD1 and KLRB1 in plasma, uncorrected for ten assays: a hypothesis, not a result.

5g. HLA, KIR and C4 against the plasma proteome

Background: plasma proteomics (Olink), NPX and pQTLs

Olink Explore HT measures ~5,400 proteins in a drop of blood plasma using a proximity extension assay: two antibodies, each carrying a DNA tag, must bind the same protein before the tags can pair; the paired tags are then counted. The result is NPX on a log2 scale. A person with a gene variant that changes a protein's plasma level is said to have a pQTL (protein quantitative trait locus).

Plasma holds the soluble fraction of a protein, not what sits on a cell's surface. And because the readout depends on antibodies, a variant can change the signal without changing the amount (an epitope effect). That is why our gene-to-protein controls matter: KIR3DL1 gene content predicts its own protein with z ≈ 51 and C4B copy number predicts C4B protein, so the pipeline sees real biology.

144 exposures (2-field HLA alleles with ≥40 carriers, C4A/C4B copy number, KIR gene content) against 5,415 Olink assays in 8,884 unrelated long-read people, adjusted for age, sex, ancestry and ten PCs. Calibration is good (genomic inflation 1.10; permutation threshold |z| ≥ 5.9). The gene-to-protein controls are strong: KIR3DL1 gene content predicts soluble KIR3DL1 (z 51), KIR2DL2/2DL3 content the KIR2DL2/3 assay (z +29/−28), C4B copy number C4B protein (z 29), and every top hit keeps its sign in all five ancestries. This validates the long-read KIR and C4 calls against an independent measurement. HLA-allele effects on HLA proteins themselves (e.g. A*01:01 → HLA-A, z 47) are expected binding/epitope effects. A UK Biobank atlas of HLA effects on plasma proteins exists (Krishna et al. 2024, 45,000 Europeans, 2,940 proteins); what this adds is five ancestries, long-read-resolved alleles, KIR content and C4 copy number on a 5,415-assay panel.

5h. HLA-B*14:02 and three unrelated proteins

Background: haplotypes, linkage, trans effects, and what 'z' and 'SD' mean here

A haplotype is a stretch of one chromosome inherited as a block. In the MHC region alleles of HLA-B, HLA-C and DRB1 are tightly linked, so when one looks associated, its neighbours do too; conditional analysis (asking whether an allele still matters after accounting for another) separates the driver from its passengers.

SD (standard deviation) is a person-to-person spread; '+2.6 SD' means carriers sit far above nearly everyone else. z is the effect divided by its uncertainty (|z| above ~6 survives the correction for testing thousands of proteins). The B*14:02 effect is trans: the allele is on chromosome 6, the three proteins are encoded on chromosomes 4, 12 and 17, and in blood the three genes are not more active, which is what makes it puzzling.

The strongest non-HLA signal: B*14:02 carriers (n = 456) sit about +2.6 SD for LRPAP1, +1.7 SD for LRP1 and +1.4 SD for SGSH (z 79, 44, 36). Dissection: it is allele-specific (B*14:01 about half the effect, +0.9 SD; C*08:01 null; C*08:02 largely tags B*14:02: both present in 399 people, joint model B*14:02 1.74 SD, C*08:02 1.20 SD); the same size in AFR (2.7 SD), AMR (2.9) and EUR (2.5), whose haplotype backgrounds differ; no single HLA-B or HLA-C residue explains it beyond B*14:02 itself; and it is specific: only 8 of 5,415 assays pass the family-wise threshold, the rest of the LRP1-ligand, receptor and lysosomal-enzyme set on the panel is flat (all |z| < 2.1). Blood RNA for the three genes does not move (8,000 people, 447 carriers; p 0.13–0.68) although the haplotype shows its expected cis effects (C4A/C4B +0.16, p < 1e-30; HLA-C −0.15).

Status: open, partly known. The UK Biobank paper reports HLA explaining about 43% of LRPAP1, 29% of SGSH and 25% of LRP1 plasma variance, calls the LRPAP1 link "both unclear and intriguing", and names no allele. Our contribution is attribution to B*14:02, sub-allele and residue resolution, and replication outside Europe. The cause is unresolved: a shared plasma interferent or antibody cross-reactivity (the three proteins also co-vary in non-carriers, r 0.15–0.40) versus a real extracellular mechanism that blood RNA cannot see. An orthogonal LRPAP1 measurement (ELISA or mass spectrometry) in B*14:02 carriers would decide it.

7. What the think tanks produced (themes, not claims)

  • Moonshots: HLA-F × KIR3DS1 epistasis; HLA-A deletion as NK-ligand compensation; parent-of-origin HLA expression; balancing-selection halos; MICA intron-5 STR as an expression switch; C4 as an interferon dial (tested: null).
  • Secure claims: native-vs-long-read benchmark (done: it is a resolution paper); phased MHC haplotype atlas; trio Mendelian error per gene; KIR gene-content atlas with a measured short-read false-negative rate; IPD submission of the validated HLA-F alleles.
  • Naive looks: ten 10-minute tables and plots (template-distance histograms, KIR gene-count outliers, DRB3/4/5 co-occurrence gaps, warning rate by sequencing date, block lengths).
  • Automated search: allele × phecode PheWAS with permutation nulls and ≥50 negative-control phenotypes; residue-level scan; haplotype-window scan; anomaly detection on per-person divergence.
  • Power-aware disease designs: residue/pocket burden two-stage (535k → 12k), blood counts and labs before disease, drug-hypersensitivity positive controls (abacavir/B*57:01, carbamazepine/B*15:02), COVID severity from predicted epitope load with a negative-control proteome.

8. Lessons that now run the project

  • A concordance number without a resolution definition is not an error rate (DQA1 0.52 vs 0.99).
  • "Novel" without a reference-release date is database age (S08).
  • Gene-level TPM validates some KIR genes and not others; controls decide which tests are allowed.
  • Quantitative traits (RNA) have power that binary EHR labels (<20 carriers) do not.
