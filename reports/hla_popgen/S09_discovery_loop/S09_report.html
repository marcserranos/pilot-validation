5. Second wave: raw reads, landscape, mechanisms

5a. Raw long-read evidence for the unnamed alleles

For 26 candidate unnamed signatures we located the exact GRCh38 position from the GENCODE CDS model (checked against the assembly bases) and piled up the raw AoU long-read alignments, independent of the assembly the call came from, in up to 8 carriers and 8 matched non-carriers. 18 of 26 are supported in every examined carrier and in 0 of 8 controls: HLA-F T237I and P205T, T279I, G128R, G37S, V309I, A322T, M119I, Q247R, three recurrent synonymous signatures, and five HLA-F loss-of-function alleles (stop-gains E70*, Q75*, W265*, R294*; start-loss M1V; each in fewer than 20 unrelated people). The HLA-E (Y28*, G321*) and HLA-G (R181*, Y142*) stop-gain calls and DPA1 E210G have no raw-read support: assembly artefacts. Three “supported” signatures (HLA-C and HLA-G synonymous, DRB1 S29A) are uninformative because the alt base is in every control. So HLA-F, not E or G, is where real protein-truncating variation sits.

5b. Do the HLA-F loss-of-function alleles lower their own transcript?

One heterozygous carrier per allele on a personal two-haplotype reference: the stop-gain R294* haplotype carries only 26% of informative HLA-F reads (bias-corrected log2 −1.23, ≈2.3× lower; consistent with nonsense-mediated decay), the start-loss M1V 40% (transcript persists, translation lost), and the missense variants 35–50%. Single-carrier evidence only.

5c. HLA-E, F and G: diversity landscape by ancestry

At equal sample size (400 unrelated people per ancestry): HLA-E is balanced (expected heterozygosity 0.50–0.52 in all six ancestries — the stable R107G polymorphism); HLA-F is nearly monomorphic and ancestry-structured (top allele 96% in EAS, 80% in AFR); HLA-G carries 4–5× more null/questionable-suffix haplotypes in AFR (6.7%) than in SAS or EUR (1.4–1.6%). For scale, HLA-B has 56–86 distinct 2-field alleles.

5d. A mechanism that failed, and one that did not

HLA-B*27 does not induce an unfolded-protein-response signature in blood RNA (UPR module β = −0.016 ± 0.024, p = 0.49, n = 5,642; ERAP1/2, IL23A, BiP, XBP1 all null; B*07, B*57, housekeeping and T-cell controls flat): a well-powered human test of the 1999 misfolding hypothesis in whole blood, informative as a null.

HLA-E R107G (E*01:03) lowers NKG2A (KLRC1) expression per NK cell. The effect is specific to KLRC1, independent of NK abundance, interferon tone, CD8/γδ T-cell content, KLRC2 (adaptive-NK proxy) and the HLA leader peptides, has the same sign in all five ancestries, is not mediated by HLA-E mRNA, and is near-dominant (heterozygote β −0.144, homozygote −0.159). No prior report was found (closest: ~100–200 donors, no effect on NKG2A frequency); novelty is unverified.

Not yet excluded. KLRC1 cis SNPs and KLRC2 deletion (need genotypes), CMV serostatus (adaptive NK), and bulk-RNA cell-type attribution. The leader −21M peptide-supply hypothesis is only nominal (meta p ≈ 0.02, attenuated by NK abundance).

5e. Hypothesis-free association scan

The HLA / KIR / C4 x ICD-10 phenome scan (594 outcome categories with at least 30 cases in 11,856 unrelated long-read people; score tests with ancestry, sex and age; negative-control outcome chapters; permutation thresholds) first failed its own calibration check because of a numerical problem with near-constant common alleles; the corrected rerun is described below if finished.

7. What the think tanks produced (themes, not claims)

  • Moonshots: HLA-F × KIR3DS1 epistasis; HLA-A deletion as NK-ligand compensation; parent-of-origin HLA expression; balancing-selection halos; MICA intron-5 STR as an expression switch; C4 as an interferon dial (tested: null).
  • Secure claims: native-vs-long-read benchmark (done: it is a resolution paper); phased MHC haplotype atlas; trio Mendelian error per gene; KIR gene-content atlas with a measured short-read false-negative rate; IPD submission of the validated HLA-F alleles.
  • Naive looks: ten 10-minute tables and plots (template-distance histograms, KIR gene-count outliers, DRB3/4/5 co-occurrence gaps, warning rate by sequencing date, block lengths).
  • Automated search: allele × phecode PheWAS with permutation nulls and ≥50 negative-control phenotypes; residue-level scan; haplotype-window scan; anomaly detection on per-person divergence.
  • Power-aware disease designs: residue/pocket burden two-stage (535k → 12k), blood counts and labs before disease, drug-hypersensitivity positive controls (abacavir/B*57:01, carbamazepine/B*15:02), COVID severity from predicted epitope load with a negative-control proteome.

8. Lessons that now run the project

  • A concordance number without a resolution definition is not an error rate (DQA1 0.52 vs 0.99).
  • "Novel" without a reference-release date is database age (S08).
  • Gene-level TPM validates some KIR genes and not others; controls decide which tests are allowed.
  • Quantitative traits (RNA) have power that binary EHR labels (<20 carriers) do not.
