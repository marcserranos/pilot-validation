Are the 'novel' HLA alleles real, and do they matter?

At a glance

What this sprint asked: 8,015 HLA calls that the annotation pipeline labelled "new" in about 12,200 long-read All of Us genomes: are they real alleles, and do they matter? Approach: try to break each claim with raw reads, short reads, platforms, ancestry, relatives, RNA and the current reference database.

Most important or most novel findings

  1. Most of the "novel" HLA calls are not new alleles. Strong Of 8,015 calls labelled novel, 86% of the novel-protein ones are indel-only (mostly one-base errors in repeat runs); only 4% of indel calls are supported by short reads, and indel "novelty" is 1.6 to 3.2 times as high on the older Sequel II/IIe platform as on Revio. See section 2.
  2. A sizeable minority of the remaining "novelty" is simply the age of the reference database. Strong Re-checking against the current IPD release (3.66, eleven releases newer than the 3.55 used for the calls) already names 62 of 441 substitution signatures (14%) and 16 of the 49 that recur in two or more people (33%), including the big East-Asian HLA-G cluster (= G*01:01:33). Indel error (item 1) is the bigger effect. See section 3.
  3. Two recurrent signatures survive every test we could run: HLA-F T237I and HLA-F P205T. Supported Unnamed in IPD 3.66, each sits on a single flanking haplotype (as the known controls do), shows no platform or centre skew, is shared by relatives where a relative exists, and is transcribed in blood (variant haplotype carries 54% and 65% of HLA-F RNA reads; a known-allele control gives 58%). They are probably real alleles, not proven: a haplotype-specific assembly error would look the same. See section 5.
  4. The recurrent variants follow ancestry. Supported That rules out random noise and points to inherited, haplotype-linked variants in groups that IPD under-represents, so "novel vs 3.55" partly measures reference bias. It cannot by itself rule out a systematic error tied to one haplotype. See section 4.

Most promising lines of research (in order)

  1. Build the IPD submission package for HLA-F T237I and P205T. The only candidate new alleles in the whole exercise. Not yet submittable. The S09 raw-read pile-up (script 105) already supports both variants in every examined carrier (up to 8; whether they are unrelated was not re-checked). Still missing: an independent trio and phased full-length exons 2–3. A trio is what would turn "probably real" into "real".
  2. Find out what the two HLA-F variants do. They sit in exon 4 (α3 domain, outside the peptide groove, so no effect on peptide presentation is expected; function is unknown). Open conformers of HLA-F bind the KIR3DS1 receptor; whether these residues matter is untested. Power is the limit: carrier counts are small (the S09 power check estimated under 50), so disease associations cannot be tested yet.
  3. Adopt the filter that makes "novel" mean novel. Match against the current IPD release, drop suffix-flagged reference alleles and calls with more than ~15 edits, and require short-read support. This took 337 shortlisted calls to 98 defensible ones (mostly HLA-F, HLA-E and DQA1).
  4. Get frequencies and transmission from outside this cohort. Population frequencies (gnomAD/ALFA) for the two HLA-F variants and trio data from the S06 yak tables; both are open items in the S08 log.

Underwhelming or negative (still informative)

  • The transmission test is unusable here: only 827 of 12,233 long-read people have a relative in the cohort, so carrier children with two long-read parents are under 20 per signature. Not feasible
  • The reference-bias proxy is inconclusive: novel-substitution burden rises with how far a person's haplotypes are from the reference, but that is what both real alleles and mapping artefacts would do. Inconclusive
  • No phenotype link yet: the health-record path was never probed, and rare alleles have almost no power anyway.
  • Several recurrent "novel" calls turned out known or artefactual (DQB1 A57D = DQB1*02:180, HLA-E K195R = E*01:152, DRB1 R262T an artefact). The shrinking of the novel list is itself the main result of this sprint, not a failure of it.
Cheat-sheet Terms used in this report (30 seconds)
Novel call
An allele that Immuannot could not match to any named allele of IPD-IMGT/HLA release 3.55.
Indel / substitution
An inserted-or-deleted base versus a changed base. One-base indels in repeat runs are the classic long-read error.
Signature
One specific change on one reference allele (for example "F T237I on F*01:01:01:01"); recurrence means the same signature in several people.
IPD / 3.55 / 3.66
The reference database of named HLA alleles and its release numbers.
Haplotype
A stretch of one chromosome inherited as a block; a real inherited allele sits on one or two backgrounds, a recurring artefact on many.
Carrier / control
A person with the signature / matched people without it.
Immuannot
The annotation tool that matches each assembled HLA/KIR gene to named IPD alleles and labels the rest "new".
Burden
Average number of calls of a given kind per person in a group.
Homopolymer
A run of identical bases (AAAAA); long reads often get its length wrong by one, which creates a fake indel.
Rebuildable
The call's alignment can be reconstructed from the public reference allele and Immuannot's edit string, so its exact changes are known.
Tier-A / suffix-flagged
Tier-A: the short-read check supports the call. Suffix-flagged: the reference allele carries an N/Q/S null or questionable suffix, so calls made against it are unreliable.
Effective backgrounds
How many genuinely different neighbouring haplotypes the carriers sit on (1 = all the same).
Bias-corrected log2
RNA-read ratio of the variant copy to the other copy, corrected with simulated reads for how well each copy aligns; 0 = balanced.
Counts <20
Group sizes below 20 are never shown (written "<20") to protect participants.
Independent read A fresh reviewer ranked this report before seeing its overview: where they agree and disagree
  1. The HLA-F T237I / P205T result is "the only discovery-like result", consistent with real alleles but not proof.
  2. The indel-error finding is the strongest result (two independent lines: short reads and platform) and explains most of the 8,015.
  3. Named recurrent signatures (G*01:01:33, F*01:25, E*01:152, DQB1*02:180) show that part of the "novelty" is reference age and bias, but the reviewer judged that effect overstated in the first draft (62/441 is 14%).

1. What 'novel' means and how it was tested Method

Bottom line'Novel' only means 'no match in the reference snapshot used'. Every claim in this report comes from taking Immuannot's own alignment of each novel call, rebuilding it from the public reference allele, classifying the edits, and then asking independent questions: do short reads agree, does it follow the sequencing platform, does it follow ancestry, is it already named in the current database, do relatives share it, is it transcribed.

Background What is an HLA allele name, and why does a database release matter?

HLA genes are the most variable in the genome; each version is an allele named with up to four fields (first two change the protein). IPD-IMGT/HLA is the reference list, updated several times a year. A variant that is rare in the European donors who dominated early releases can be perfectly real and still look 'novel' until a later release names it.

Background What raw reads versus an assembly mean

Reads are short pieces of sequence; software stitches them into an assembly, and alleles are called from the assembly. An error in the assembly (most often a missing or extra base in a run of identical bases) creates a 'novel allele' no read supports. Checking the raw reads is the cleanest test.

How it was done Scripts and data

90_case_browser*.py (viewer and per-case alignments), 91_novel_recurrence.py (signatures, ancestry, relatives, matching against IPD), 92_novel_burden.py (platform × ancestry), 93_shortlist_ipd_release.py, 95_signature_context.py (haplotype origin, centre skew), 96_f_signature_ase.py (RNA allele-specific expression). VM runs used one core at low priority from a throwaway clone that was removed afterwards; raw tables and carrier lists are mode 600 on the VM and not in the repository.

2. Most 'novel' calls are indel errors Strong

Bottom lineLow-distance 'novel' calls are mostly consensus errors, not new alleles. 86% of novel-protein calls are indels only, almost all one base long and often in runs of identical bases; only 4% of them are supported by independent short reads; and they are about 1.6 to 3.2 times as common on the older sequencing platform (Sequel II/IIe, about 0.9k of the 12,233 people). Substitution-only calls show no consistent platform pattern.

Evidence Detail from the case browser
  • All 249 ladder alignments replay Immuannot's own alignment string exactly.
  • Indels do not recur at fixed positions (462 distinct sites among 486 events, 23 recurrent), are spread uniformly along the coding sequence, and 41 of 140 indel-carrying cases restore the reading frame with a second indel: the pattern of sequencing error, not of mutation.
  • Mendelian check (30 sampled mismatches): 17 were within 6 edits of a parent haplotype, and the child's own call was 0 edits from the parent in all 17.
Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • The 762 browser cases are stratified examples, not population rates; the whole-cohort statistics (8,015 calls) come from the separate stats pass.
  • Short-read verdicts can't check DRB3/DRB4 (no primary-assembly locus).
  • Platform effects were tested for the classical genes only.

How it was done Denominators: why 8,015, 7,635, 682 and 675 differ
  • 8,015 novel calls with a coding-sequence alignment = 7,635 novel-protein + 227 novel-synonymous + 153 beyond the coding sequence (137 further calls could not be rebuilt from the edit string).
  • 682 substitution-only calls come from the case-browser statistics pass (script 90); 675 substitution-only and rebuildable come from the recurrence script (91). They count slightly differently; the 7-call gap is consistent with the rebuildability requirement but was not traced call by call.
  • 441 distinct signatures = distinct (gene, reference allele, set of substitutions) among the 675.
  • 337 shortlist = protein-altering substitution-only calls kept for manual QC.
Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • '*' in a partial IPD entry means an unsequenced position, which was not resolved.
  • The 3.66 re-check was run from the public nucleotide file; names are the same ones IPD would assign for exact coding matches, but the WHO committee assigns official names.
  • The DQB1 A57D = 02:180 naming rests on a literature check that has not been re-derived locally.

4. The recurrent signatures follow ancestry Supported

Bottom lineInherited, haplotype-linked variants of under-represented groups, not discoveries of new biology. Recurrent signatures are concentrated in a few ancestries (for example the HLA-G cluster is almost entirely East Asian; HLA-F T237I and P205T are mostly African-ancestry), and each sits on a single reference allele. Per person, novelty is highest in AFR and EAS and lowest in EUR.
49 of 441signatures seen in ≥2 unrelated people
1 referenceallele under every recurrent signature
AFR, EAShighest novelty per person
Background Why ancestry structure points to real variants

A random error would appear in every ancestry at about the same rate and on many different backgrounds, so ancestry structure and a single reference allele rule out random noise. They do not rule out a systematic error tied to one haplotype or to a poorly represented reference template, which would also be ancestry-structured and sit on one background. The pattern therefore supports 'inherited, haplotype-linked' but not, by itself, 'real allele'.

Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • Ancestry labels are AoU predictions.
  • Unrelated carriers are counted after removing relatives; group sizes under 20 are not shown.

5. HLA-F T237I and P205T: the two alleles that survive Strong

Bottom lineBoth are probably real inherited alleles, unnamed in IPD and transcribed; this is supported, not proven. Each sits on a single flanking haplotype, matches the ancestry-expected number of carriers per sequencing platform and centre, is shared by relatives wherever a relative exists, and is expressed in blood RNA (54% and 65% of HLA-F reads from the variant copy; a known allele control gives 58%). Both lie in exon 4 (α3 domain), outside the peptide-binding groove, so no effect on peptide presentation is expected and their function is unknown. A haplotype-specific assembly or template error would also give a single background, which is why raw-read confirmation in unrelated carriers and a trio are still needed.

Evidence Batch effects and relatives

Platform and sequencing centre: observed carriers per platform and per centre match the ancestry-matched expectation for all four signatures (for example Revio 22 observed vs 22.1 expected for HLA-F T237I; the Broad centre 86 vs 85.7 for the G control). One centre is mildly above expectation for T237I, not significant.

Relatives: where a relative is in the long-read cohort the relative carries the same signature in almost every case (counts under 20 are withheld). Consistent with inheritance but too few for a formal transmission rate.

Evidence Quality checks on each signature
  • Each signature is always against a single reference allele, strands are balanced, mapping quality is ≈60 and there is no CpG context (a hypermutable site type). Short-read support is described in the log as 'mostly' clean; no per-signature count was recorded.
  • 'Matched non-carrier' haplotypes are drawn from people of the same HLA-F lineage and ancestry (500 draws); 'expected' carriers per platform/centre come from the ancestry-matched share of that platform/centre.
  • HLA-F and HLA-E alleles are absent from the only local IPD FASTA (3.38), so cross-allele checks used the public 3.66 alignment files.
Caveats and red-team What could still be wrong
  • Still missing for a submission: an independent trio and phased full-length exons. (The S09 raw-read pile-up supports both variants in every examined carrier, up to 8; relatedness among them was not re-checked.)
  • Fewer than 20 carriers per signature have RNA, so the expression result rests on few people (exact counts withheld).

6. What it would take to claim a new allele Open question

Bottom lineIPD-IMGT/HLA requires phased, full-length exons (2–3 for class I; 2 for class II), the new allele sequenced apart from its partner, and an official name assigned by the WHO Nomenclature Committee. The transmission evidence that would normally help is not available in this cohort.
827 / 12,233long-read people with a relative in the cohort
~41%of assembled HLA/KIR sequences novel vs IPD 3.52 in the Immuannot paper (mostly non-classical)
trioan independent parent-child trio is the main missing piece
Evidence The think-tank consensus (what we would do next, in order)
  1. Transmission oracle across platforms (child long-read vs parental short-read, because same-pipeline errors transmit).
  2. Simulated reference-bias null.
  3. RNA-seq allele-specific expression on personal references (done, section 5).
  4. Primate outgroup (can only confirm antiquity).
  5. Per-run error barcode for hidden batch effects.
  6. Mark-recapture per-platform error estimate.

Check trio × carrier × RNA overlap before committing to any of them: power may be near zero for HLA-F T237I/P205T.

Caveats and red-team Honest limits
  • Novelty was defined against IPD 3.55; 3.66 changes the answer and 3.67 will again.
  • Signatures are keyed on one reference allele; ancestry labels are AoU predictions; relatedness data is sparse.
  • No phenotype comparison: the health-record path was not probed and rare alleles have little power.
