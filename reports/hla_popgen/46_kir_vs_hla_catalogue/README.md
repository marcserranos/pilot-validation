## Question

Which region — KIR (IPD-KIR 2.13.0) or HLA (IPD-IMGT/HLA 3.55.0) — is better represented by its
reference catalogue in this cohort, and does the answer differ gene by gene, AND by identity level
(any-level vs. protein-level)? Per `ORCHESTRATOR_HANDOFF.md` WS-B and the 2026-09-27 coordinator
follow-up, using the same unrelated set, ancestry scheme, subsampling and recurrence classes as
44/45 (never re-derived).

## Method

All metrics are computed per gene, pooled ALL ancestry, from 44's own already-disclosure-handled
`coverage_chao2.tsv`, `equal_n_slope.tsv`, and (new) `kir_protein_catalogue_qc.tsv`:

- **`pct_novel_any`** = 100 × S_obs(any-level-novel) / S_obs(genomic) — % of a gene's distinct
  alleles that carry any sequence difference from the reference catalogue at all.
- **`pct_novel_protein`** = 100 × S_obs(protein-level-novel) / S_obs(**protein**) — the
  protein-level analogue, now using the SAME identity granularity for numerator and denominator
  (fixed 2026-09-27: `protein_novel` is by construction a subset of `protein`, so this ratio can
  only exceed 100% from a genuine remaining bug, never a granularity mismatch — see "Resolved"
  note in `build_gene_metrics()`'s own docstring).
- **`good_turing_coverage`** — incidence coverage (Chao & Jost 2012), reused verbatim from 44,
  genomic level.
- **`completeness`** = S_obs(genomic) / Chao2(genomic) — Chao2 (1987) richness completeness at the
  GENOMIC level.
- **`completeness_protein`** = S_obs(protein) / Chao2(protein) — the SAME richness-completeness
  statistic computed entirely within the protein identity granularity (new 2026-09-27, direct
  answer to "which catalogue is better represented AT THE PROTEIN LEVEL").
- **`chao2_undetected_f0hat`** — Chao2's estimated count of still-unseen alleles (genomic level).
- **`slope_per_1000`** — equal-N discovery slope (genomic level, pooled ALL ancestry), reused
  verbatim from 44's `equal_n_slope.tsv` (see 44's README Caveats for a flagged, pre-existing,
  unrelated inconsistency between this file and 44's own README prose — not used as a headline
  number here for that reason).
- **`protein_catalogue_covered` / `protein_catalogue_reason`** — joined from
  `kir_protein_catalogue_qc.tsv` (KIR only; HLA genes default to `covered=True, reason="ok"` since
  every HLA classical gene has a curated IPD-IMGT/HLA protein entry). **KIR2DP1 and KIR3DP1 are
  `covered=False, reason="no_catalogued_protein_entries"`** (both pseudogenes) — their
  `pct_novel_protein`/`completeness_protein` are NaN, rendered EXPLICITLY in the figure (see
  below), never as 0 or silently dropped.

The paired recurrence-class composition figure reuses
`45_kir_recurrence_figure.pooled_recurrence_from_curves()` (imported by path, not re-derived) so
this script's recurrence split can never silently disagree with 45's — both read the same
curve-derived, exactly-partitioning pooled counts (see 45's README section "Reconciliation note").

### Interpretation guard (repeated from 45 — do not violate when writing prose from this figure)

Good–Turing incidence coverage and Chao2 richness completeness answer **different questions** and
must never be collapsed into one "coverage" number:
- Incidence coverage (≥99% for both species) says "if we sampled one more carrier, there's a 99%+
  chance their allele has already been seen" — dominated by common alleles, says almost nothing
  about total diversity.
- Chao2 completeness (at either identity level) says "this fraction of the estimated TOTAL allele
  richness has been observed" — the number that actually answers WS-B's question, and it gives a
  DIFFERENT ANSWER at the genomic level (KIR ahead) than at the protein level (HLA ahead) — see
  below.

**Never write "99% of the allele space is explored."** That conflates the two metrics.

## Results

Per-gene metrics: `46_catalogue_metrics.tsv` (25 rows: 8 HLA classical genes + 17 KIR genes).

**At the GENOMIC level, KIR's catalogue is better represented than HLA's**, both pooled (44's own
finding: 84.2% vs. 63.7% Chao2 completeness) and gene-by-gene (mean per-gene completeness 86.8%
KIR vs. 66.9% HLA) — despite KIR carrying a much higher raw novelty rate (mean 54.8% vs. 29.5%
any-level-novel). Not a contradiction: KIR's excess genomic-level novelty is disproportionately
*shared* across unrelated people (45's singleton-share test), inflating Chao2's numerator (S_obs)
as much as its denominator.

**At the PROTEIN level, this reverses: HLA's catalogue is better represented than KIR's**
(pooled: HLA 39.9% vs. KIR 15.1% completeness; mean per-gene: HLA 40.5% vs. KIR 15.6%). See
"Protein-level reversal" below.

**`fig_catalogue_completeness.png/.pdf`** — two panels, both x = % novel, y = Chao2 completeness,
KIR (navy) vs. HLA (red), direct gene labels (`check_layout(strict=True)`-clean):
- **Panel a (any-level identity)**: HLA genes cluster lower-left (lower novelty, lower
  completeness), KIR genes upper-right (higher novelty, higher completeness) — the genomic-level
  finding above. KIR2DS2/KIR3DS1 (~65% completeness) are the KIR outliers nearest the HLA cluster;
  HLA-DQA1 (85.0% completeness) is the HLA outlier nearest the KIR cluster.
- **Panel b (protein-level identity)**: the picture inverts. HLA's 8 genes sit at LOW novelty
  (13-38%) and MODERATE completeness (22-58%); all 15 protein-catalogue-covered KIR genes sit at
  VERY HIGH novelty (91-99%) and LOW completeness (8-24%) — a tight cluster in the bottom-right,
  labeled with a shared-column leader-line layout (not free per-point labels: 13 KIR genes packed
  into a small area made free labels sit on marker dots even though `check_layout()`'s
  text-vs-text check found zero violations — a real "text on data" fault the mechanical linter
  cannot catch, only caught by rendering and viewing the PNG at full size). **KIR2DP1 and KIR3DP1
  are named explicitly in an in-panel note ("pseudogenes, no catalogued reference protein
  (excluded, not 0)")** rather than silently vanishing from the plot or appearing as points at 0.

**Per-gene highlights, genomic level** (from `46_catalogue_metrics.tsv`):
- **Worst-represented**: HLA-C (50.2% completeness) and HLA-A (54.6%) — the two most extensively
  catalogued classical HLA genes historically are, in THIS cohort's Chao2 estimate, the least
  complete; consistent with AoU's ancestry mix exposing gaps in the genes with the richest
  worldwide allelic diversity.
- **Best-represented HLA gene**: HLA-DQA1 (85.0% completeness, lowest any-level novelty at 27.1%).
- **Worst-represented KIR genes**: KIR2DS2 (65.3%) and KIR3DS1 (65.5%).
- **Best-represented KIR genes**: KIR2DL2 (97.6%), KIR2DS1 (96.9%), KIR2DL5B (96.9%).

### Protein-level reversal: does KIR still look better represented? **No.**

| species | mean pct_novel_protein (per gene) | mean completeness_protein (per gene) | pooled pct_novel_protein | pooled completeness_protein |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HLA | 20.7% | **40.5%** | 18.4% | **39.9%** |
| KIR (15 covered genes) | 94.6% | **15.6%** | 94.3% | **15.1%** |

At the protein level, EVERY SINGLE KIR gene has lower completeness than every single HLA gene
(panel b's two clusters do not overlap on the y-axis at all: KIR spans 7.6-24.4%, HLA spans
21.6-57.3%). This is the opposite of the genomic-level ranking. **Mechanism flagged as an open
question, not asserted as settled** (no VM access from this local-only task to inspect raw
per-person translations): KIR's `protein` identity is a hash of the translated CDS sequence per
haplotype; if some of the non-coding-dominated novelty that drives KIR's genomic-level excess
(S03: 58.9% of KIR calls novel, mostly non-coding) still perturbs the translated hash without
representing a biologically real amino-acid-level allele, Chao2's rare-class counts (`f1`/`f2`)
would be inflated far beyond genuine protein diversity — a milder, not-yet-fully-eliminated version
of the same class of bug already fixed for KIR2DL2/KIR2DL5B/KIR2DP1 in v3. Equally possible: KIR's
translated-protein diversity genuinely and substantially exceeds what IPD-KIR 2.13.0 catalogues,
a real biological/database-maturity difference. **The NUMBER (15.1% vs. 39.9%) is reproducible
from the committed v3 TSVs; only the MECHANISM is uncertain** — flagged for Marc/Aleix, not
decided here, per this repo's disclosure/uncertainty convention.

**`fig_recurrence_composition.png/.pdf`** — paired recurrence-class composition bars (share of
distinct novel alleles seen 1x / 2x / >2x, which already includes ≥20x), KIR vs. HLA, any-level
vs. protein-level, using 45's corrected pooled counts (now real at protein level for HLA too).
**How to read**: panel a (any-level) — KIR's any-level novelty is 80.2% shared (19.8% singleton)
vs. HLA's 58.0% shared (42.0% singleton), per 45's z-test (p≈1e-21). Panel b (protein-level, now
real data for both species) — BOTH species are overwhelmingly singleton-dominated (HLA 97.0%, KIR
86.5%), the opposite composition from panel a's KIR pattern. **Read this as: KIR's bulk
non-coding/any-level novelty looks like real, shared, catalogue-missing diversity; genuine
protein-level novelty in BOTH species looks private/ordinary-rare-variant-like.** This is the
recurrence-side evidence consistent with the Chao2-completeness reversal above: if KIR's
protein-level "novelty" were mostly systematic catalogue gaps (like its any-level novelty), it
would be mostly shared, not 86.5% private.

## Caveats

- **Resolved in this pass (was flagged here previously)**: `pct_novel_protein`'s denominator now
  uses `protein` S_obs (not `genomic`) — fixes the previous KIR>100% artifact structurally, not
  just for the 3 genes 44 itself fixed. HLA's `protein_novel`/`completeness_protein` are real,
  non-zero numbers now that 44's CDS-path bug is fixed.
- **KIR2DP1 and KIR3DP1 have no catalogued reference protein in IPD-KIR 2.13.0** (both
  pseudogenes, `kir_protein_catalogue_qc.tsv`) — `pct_novel_protein`/`completeness_protein` are
  NaN for these two genes, rendered as an explicit in-panel note in `fig_catalogue_completeness`
  panel b, never as 0 and never silently dropped from the point count.
- **The protein-level KIR-vs-HLA reversal's mechanism is unconfirmed** (see "Protein-level
  reversal" above) — could be a real biological/catalogue-maturity difference or a residual,
  milder identity-hashing artifact of the same class fixed for 3 KIR genes in 44's v3. The NUMBER
  is reproducible and real; the INTERPRETATION needs a VM-side check of raw translations before
  being treated as a settled finding for the paper.
- `coverage_chao2.tsv`'s `s_obs` column is not `<20`-masked per gene (unlike
  `recurrence_classes.tsv`'s allele-class counts) — several KIR per-gene s_obs values used here
  are below 20 (e.g. KIR2DL5A genomic=25, KIR2DS1 genomic=22). Used as committed by 44 (re-masking
  another script's already-committed aggregate output is out of scope here); flagged per
  CLAUDE.md's disclosure guard, not decided — Marc/Aleix should confirm this is consistent with
  44's own stated `<20` masking policy before this figure or its per-gene numbers go outside the
  team.
- `pct_novel_any`/`completeness`/`good_turing_coverage`/protein-level equivalents are all
  pooled-ALL-ancestry; per-ancestry per-gene breakdowns exist in 44's own TSVs but are not
  re-plotted here (would be ~150+ points per panel across 6 ancestries × 25 genes).
- `slope_per_1000` is reported in the TSV but not plotted in either figure — 44's README flags a
  pre-existing, unrelated inconsistency between `equal_n_slope.tsv` and 44's own README prose;
  this script reports the raw per-gene TSV value without attempting to resolve that.

## Distilled

- **The "which catalogue is better represented" answer DEPENDS ON THE IDENTITY LEVEL.** At the
  genomic level, KIR wins (84.2% vs. 63.7% pooled Chao2 completeness; 86.8% vs. 66.9% mean
  per-gene) because its excess novelty is mostly shared, catalogue-missing non-coding variation.
  **At the protein level, HLA wins by more than 2x (39.9% vs. 15.1% pooled; 40.5% vs. 15.6% mean
  per-gene)** — every single KIR gene has lower protein-level completeness than every single HLA
  gene. Mechanism (real biology vs. residual identity-hashing artifact) is flagged, not resolved.
- Worst-represented (genomic): HLA-C (50.2%), HLA-A (54.6%); KIR2DS2 (65.3%), KIR3DS1 (65.5%).
  Best (genomic): HLA-DQA1 (85.0%); KIR2DL2 (97.6%).
- **KIR2DP1 and KIR3DP1 have no catalogued protein at all** (pseudogenes) — rendered as an
  explicit named note in the figure, never as 0.
- **Recurrence composition flips too**: KIR's any-level novelty is 80.2% shared (systematic-gap
  signature); genuine protein-level novelty in BOTH species is overwhelmingly private (HLA 97.0%,
  KIR 86.5% singleton) — consistent with the completeness reversal, not contradicting it.
- Two figures (`fig_catalogue_completeness` — now 2 panels, `fig_recurrence_composition`) + one
  TSV (`46_catalogue_metrics.tsv`, now with `completeness_protein`/`protein_catalogue_covered`/
  `protein_catalogue_reason` columns), all `check_layout(strict=True)`-clean.
- Good–Turing incidence coverage (≥99% both species, genomic level) is a different, less
  informative metric than Chao2 completeness for this question — see the interpretation guard;
  never conflate the two.
