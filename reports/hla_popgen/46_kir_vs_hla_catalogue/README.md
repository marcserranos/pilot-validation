## Question

Which region — KIR (IPD-KIR 2.13.0) or HLA (IPD-IMGT/HLA 3.55.0) — is better represented by its
reference catalogue in this cohort, and does the answer differ gene by gene? Per
`ORCHESTRATOR_HANDOFF.md` WS-B, using the same unrelated set, ancestry scheme, subsampling and
recurrence classes as 44/45 (never re-derived).

## Method

All metrics are computed per gene, pooled ALL ancestry, at the **genomic** identity granularity
(44's finest-grained allele identity — the full called allele including non-coding differences)
unless noted, from 44's own already-disclosure-handled `coverage_chao2.tsv` and
`equal_n_slope.tsv`:

- **`pct_novel_any`** = 100 × S_obs(any-level-novel) / S_obs(genomic) — % of a gene's distinct
  alleles that carry any sequence difference from the reference catalogue at all.
- **`pct_novel_protein`** = 100 × S_obs(protein-level-novel) / S_obs(genomic) — nominally the
  amino-acid-changing-novelty analogue; **see the Caveats section — this number is not
  interpretable as a percentage for KIR and is reported for completeness only, never used in a
  headline claim** (it exceeds 100% for every KIR gene).
- **`good_turing_coverage`** — incidence coverage (Chao & Jost 2012), reused verbatim from 44.
- **`completeness`** = S_obs(genomic) / Chao2(genomic) — Chao2 (1987) richness completeness, the
  fraction of a gene's *estimated total* allele richness already observed in this cohort. **This
  is the number that actually answers "how well represented is this gene's catalogue"** — see the
  interpretation guard below.
- **`chao2_undetected_f0hat`** — Chao2's estimated count of still-unseen alleles for that gene.
- **`slope_per_1000`** — equal-N discovery slope (genomic level, pooled ALL ancestry), reused
  verbatim from 44's `equal_n_slope.tsv`.

The paired recurrence-class composition figure reuses
`45_kir_recurrence_figure.pooled_recurrence_from_curves()` (imported by path, not re-derived) so
this script's recurrence split can never silently disagree with 45's — both read the same
curve-derived, exactly-partitioning pooled counts (see 45's README section "Reconciliation note").

### Interpretation guard (repeated from 45 — do not violate when writing prose from this figure)

Good–Turing incidence coverage and Chao2 richness completeness answer **different questions** and
must never be collapsed into one "coverage" number:
- Incidence coverage (≥99% for both species) says "if we sampled one more carrier, there's a 99%+
  chance their allele has already been seen" — dominated by common alleles, says almost nothing
  about total diversity.
- Chao2 completeness (HLA 66.8% mean per-gene, KIR 86.8% mean per-gene — see Results) says "this
  fraction of the estimated TOTAL allele richness has been observed" — the number that actually
  answers WS-B's question.

**Never write "99% of the allele space is explored."** That conflates the two metrics.

## Results

Per-gene metrics: `46_catalogue_metrics.tsv` (25 rows: 8 HLA classical genes + 17 KIR genes).

**Headline: KIR's catalogue is better represented than HLA's, both at the pooled level (44's own
finding: 84.2% vs. 63.7% pooled Chao2 completeness) and gene-by-gene (mean per-gene completeness
86.8% KIR vs. 66.8% HLA) — despite KIR carrying a much higher raw novelty rate (54.8% vs. 29.7%
mean per-gene any-level-novel).** This is not a contradiction: KIR's excess novelty is
disproportionately *shared* across unrelated people (see "Recurrence composition" below and 45's
singleton-share test), so it inflates Chao2's numerator (S_obs) as much as its denominator
(Chao2) — a gene can have both a high novelty rate and a high completeness if most of its "novel"
alleles are the same handful of recurring, catalogue-missing variants rather than a long tail of
distinct rare ones.

**`fig_catalogue_completeness.png/.pdf`** — per-gene scatter, x = % novel (any level), y = Chao2
completeness, KIR (navy) vs. HLA (red), direct gene labels, no overlaps
(`check_layout(strict=True)`-clean). **How to read**: the two species form visually distinct
clusters — HLA genes sit in the lower-left (lower novelty, lower completeness), KIR genes in the
upper-right (higher novelty, higher completeness) — with KIR2DS2/KIR3DS1 as the two KIR outliers
closest to the HLA cluster (both ~65% completeness, the two least-well-characterized KIR genes in
this cohort) and HLA-DQA1 as the HLA outlier closest to the KIR cluster (85.7% completeness, the
best-represented HLA gene here).

**Per-gene highlights** (from `46_catalogue_metrics.tsv`):
- **Worst-represented**: HLA-C (50.5% completeness) and HLA-A (55.1%) — the two most extensively
  catalogued classical HLA genes historically are, in THIS cohort's Chao2 estimate, the least
  complete; consistent with AoU's ancestry mix (AFR/AMR-enriched relative to IPD-IMGT/HLA's
  historically EUR-heavy reference population) exposing gaps in exactly the genes with the
  richest allelic diversity worldwide.
- **Best-represented HLA gene**: HLA-DQA1 (85.7% completeness, also the lowest any-level novelty
  at 27.1% and lowest equal-N slope at 2.80/1,000 — the flattest-still-climbing HLA gene).
- **Worst-represented KIR genes**: KIR2DS2 (65.3%) and KIR3DS1 (65.5%) — both framework-adjacent
  activating receptor genes with known structural/copy-number complexity (KIR2DS2 co-occurs with
  KIR2DL2 on the cB haplotype; S03 flagged KIR2DL2/KIR2DL3 co-occurrence as a QC open item that
  may share a root cause here).
- **Best-represented KIR genes**: KIR2DL2 (97.6%), KIR2DS1 (96.9%), KIR2DL5B (96.9%) — all with
  very low equal-N slopes (≤0.2/1,000), i.e. already essentially flat.

**`fig_recurrence_composition.png/.pdf`** — paired recurrence-class composition bars (share of
distinct novel alleles seen 1x / 2x / >2x, which already includes ≥20x), KIR vs. HLA, any-level
vs. protein-level, using 45's corrected pooled counts. **How to read**: panel a (any-level) shows
the finding already reported in 45 — KIR's any-level novelty is 80% shared (only 19.8% singleton)
vs. HLA's 59% shared (40.9% singleton), statistically confirmed there (p≈6e-20). Panel b
(protein-level) is blank for HLA (0 alleles, an unreconciled 44 data question, annotated in-panel
rather than left as an unexplained empty bar) and shows KIR's OWN protein-level novelty is 88.2%
singleton — the opposite pattern from KIR's any-level novelty. **Read this as: KIR's bulk
non-coding novelty looks like real, shared, catalogue-missing diversity; KIR's much smaller pool
of genuine amino-acid-changing novelty looks private**, similar in character to ordinary rare
variation. Do not use panel b to make an HLA vs. KIR protein-level comparison — it is not
computable for HLA in this dataset.

## Caveats

- **`pct_novel_protein` is not a valid percentage for KIR and must not be used in any headline
  claim.** It exceeds 100% for every single KIR gene (e.g. KIR2DS4 = 1,648%), because
  `protein_novel`'s S_obs counts distinct novel PROTEIN-hash identities, which is not bounded by
  the GENOMIC-level S_obs it is being divided by here — the two columns come from different
  identity granularities (44's own three-granularity design, "Resolved: KIR/HLA identity
  mismatch" in 44's README) and are not a numerator/denominator pair for KIR the way they are for
  HLA (where protein_novel is a real subset of genomic alleles, currently 0). A plausible
  mechanism, NOT verified against per-person data (no VM access from this local-only task): KIR's
  novelty is 58.9% non-coding (S03 finding); a non-coding change can still land inside 44's
  `cds.fa.gz`-derived CDS window in a way that shifts the reading frame or introduces a premature
  stop at a different position per haplotype, so many genuinely-distinct-but-related non-coding
  variants could translate to many distinct "protein" hashes without representing that many real
  amino-acid-level alleles. This is flagged as an open data-quality question for 44/41, not
  something this local figure script can resolve or should paper over by silently correcting the
  ratio.
- **`coverage_chao2.tsv`'s `s_obs` column is not `<20`-masked per gene** (unlike
  `recurrence_classes.tsv`'s allele-class counts) — several KIR `any_novel`/`protein_novel` s_obs
  values used here are below 20 (e.g. KIR2DL5A=11, KIR2DS3=16, KIR2DL2=16, KIR2DS1=14, per
  `46_catalogue_metrics.tsv`). This script uses 44's committed numbers as-is (re-masking another
  script's already-committed aggregate output is out of scope here), but per CLAUDE.md's
  disclosure guard this is flagged, not decided — Marc/Aleix should confirm this is consistent
  with 44's own stated `<20` masking policy (its Disclosure section explicitly masks allele-class
  counts elsewhere) before this figure or its per-gene numbers go outside the team.
- `pct_novel_any`/`completeness`/`good_turing_coverage` are all pooled-ALL-ancestry; per-ancestry
  per-gene breakdowns exist in 44's own TSVs but are not re-plotted here (would be ~150 points per
  panel across 6 ancestries × 25 genes — a supplementary table, not this headline figure).
- `slope_per_1000` is reported in the TSV but not plotted in either figure here — it already has a
  dedicated multi-ancestry treatment in 45's `fig_saturation_per_ancestry` and 44's own
  `equal_n_slope.tsv`; repeating it as a third panel here risked diluting this figure's one clear
  message (novelty rate vs. completeness) per the "restrained, one comparison" style guidance.
- HLA `protein_novel` S_obs = 0 for every one of the 8 classical genes (not just pooled) — the
  same unreconciled 44 data question flagged in 45's README, reproduced here because it directly
  blanks `pct_novel_protein` for HLA and panel b of `fig_recurrence_composition`.
