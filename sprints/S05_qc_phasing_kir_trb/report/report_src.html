The answers in one screen

Class II haplotypes are physically reliable

In 179 trios, DQ, DR–DQ, DP and B~C contigs carry genes from two different parents in fewer than 7.7% of cases. For pairs that include HLA-A, that rate is about 40%. So haplotype claims are safe within class II and B~C, but not across HLA-A ().

Typing error is about 4% at 2 fields

Relatives and trios measure it independently and agree: 4.09% vs 4.15%. A confidence score ≥ 4 halves it while keeping 91% of calls. Third/fourth-field discordance is about 3× higher ().

The classical "novel protein" headline does not survive

4,250 protein-novel sequences drop to 0 that are recurrent, clean and common. At homopolymer indels, most HiFi reads carry the known run length. About 345 calls are frame-shift artefacts in the caller's CDS records. Long reads support a small core of 4–9 recurrent alleles ().

The DQ G1/G2 rule holds

Cross-group DQA1~DQB1 pairings appear only across contig breaks or on structurally abnormal contigs. The haplotype atlas rediscovers the DQ and DR51/52/53 rules unprompted ().

Coverage is not the problem

MHC and KIR run 12–17% below each person's genome depth in almost everyone, and true outliers are rare. Depth explains none of the bad calls, so no coverage filter is applied ().

TRBV4-3 absence is germline and ancestry-structured

No copy of TRBV4-3 in 19.5% of EUR, 33% overall, and 55% of SAS. This is the same order as the repertoire's "never used" rates, so the usage gap is first a germline deletion ().

Decisions only Marc can make

Each one is a proposal in context/DECISIONS.md. The sprint's code already supports every option.

  1. Ancestry-purity rule. Recommended: primary analyses on predicted ancestry, with p ≥ 0.95 as a sensitivity analysis. A hard p ≥ 0.95 keeps 93% of SAS but only 38% of EUR and MID ().
  2. Demote classical protein-novel counts to a resource or supplement. Name only the read-supported core (Part 5). Recommended: yes.
  3. KIR haplotype claims only on complete haplotypes (75% of haplotypes, Part 8). Recommended: yes.
  4. Physical-haplotype evidence rule: allowed for DQ, DR–DQ, DP and B~C; A~B and A~C only with statistical phasing or trios; DQ–DP flagged. Recommended: yes. Cole's units (62) already follow it.
  5. Resize the VM for the 991 Sequel II people (63). About 3 days on a bigger app vs about 27 days as is. The 2-person smoke test found a filter bug, now fixed; a second smoke test should pass before you decide.
  6. Small allele counts. Distinct-allele counts below 20 (for example "7 keys") are shown exactly here, because they count alleles, not people. Decide whether they are masked in figures you publish.

Part 2Can we trust the phased haplotypes?

Every haplotype claim (DQ G1/G2, the atlas, Cole's fine-mapping units, KIR haplotypes) assumes that genes on one contig come from one parent. Three independent checks test that assumption. First, how often genes share a contig at all (50b). Second, whether a haplotype seen in one relative appears intact in the other (56b). Third, the direct truth from trios: which parent each gene came from (57, 57b).

Part 3Can we trust the allele calls?

Typing errors are measured without trusting the caller. Relatives who share a haplotype must share an allele at every gene (56b), and a child's allele must be present in the parent who transmitted it (57b). Script 56 also builds a per-call confidence score from 0 to 7, so each claim can use only calls that are good enough. The score gets one point each for: same contig as the phasing partner; ≤ 2 MHC contigs on the haplotype; template distance ≤ cohort 75th percentile; not a homopolymer-only indel; no template warning; no tie between candidate alleles; Revio.

Part 4Coverage over the immune regions

The supervisor asked for a regional coverage filter: flag people whose MHC depth is far below their genome depth. Script 58 measured every person (12,233 people, 0 failures, 137 regions, samtools bedcov at MAPQ ≥ 0 and ≥ 20). Script 59 then asked whether thin coverage explains the calls we distrust.

Does thin coverage explain the bad calls? No.

Script 59 joined each unrelated person's MHC and KIR depth (normalised to the cohort median of the same region and platform) to eight outcome flags. The flags covered incomplete KIR haplotypes, missing KIR framework genes, absent HLA genes, empty haplotypes, DQ genes split across contigs, anomalous DQ spacing, cross-group DQ pairs and MHC fragmentation (≥ 7 contigs). It then scanned cutoffs under a rule fixed in advance: the smallest cutoff that removes ≥ 50% of an outcome's events while removing ≤ 10% of people, overall and in every ancestry with ≥ 100 people.

Part 5Novel HLA alleles: what survives

The earlier figures counted thousands of "novel" HLA alleles. Before any of them becomes a headline, three questions need answers. Do they recur in unrelated people? Do they avoid the known HiFi error class (1-bp indels in homopolymer runs)? Do the person's own reads carry them? Script 50d asked the first two. Script 64 re-matched every classical protein-novel and synonymous-novel call against IPD-IMGT/HLA 3.65.0 (July 2026, 10 releases after the 3.55.0 the caller used) and tested each against the HiFi reads.

Part 9The TRB germline insertion and TRBV4-3

Aleix found that TRBV4-3 is never used in about 27% of people's T-cell repertoires, with SAS 43.5% and EUR 16.7%. A known germline polymorphism removes TRBV4-3 together with TRBV3-2 and one copy of TRBV6-2/6-3. If a person has no copy, they cannot use the gene. That explanation must be measured before the usage gap is read as immunology. The literature describes this deletion. What is new here is ancestry-stratified long-read genotyping in about 11,000 diverse people, linked to their own repertoires.

How each person was genotyped

65 reads mode runs on everyone. For each person it takes the long reads over the TRB span. A reference read spans the empty insertion site without a break, with ≥ 1 kb anchored on each side; only a chromosome without the insertion can produce one. An insertion read carries a TRBV4-3 or TRBV3-2 probe hit. Allele fraction = insertion / (insertion + reference). Copies are 0 below 0.2, 1 between 0.2 and 0.8, and 2 above 0.8, with ≥ 6 informative reads required (the median was 14). The run covered 12,233 people in 5 h with 0 failures.

Script 60 is blocked needs Aleix

The per-person join (copies vs never used, PPV of 0 copies for non-use, dosage, residual non-use among carriers) is written and tested. It needs Aleix's W1 usage table, and that table is not on Marc's app: only per-person TRUST4 files are there. Aleix needs to copy the usage table (or vj_usage.pkl) to the bucket.

Part 10The 991 Sequel II people (mostly AFR)

None of the Sequel II people has HLA or KIR calls, which under-samples AFR in every result. The diagnosis is in SEQUEL2_DIAGNOSIS.md. Their manifest rows lack the assembly-to-GRCh38 alignments, so they fell into the "self-align" trim tier, which production skipped. That path was also broken in three ways:

  • their FASTAs are plain gzip, which samtools faidx cannot index;
  • a whole assembly aligned to chr6 alone pulled in 1.3–1.9 GB per haplotype instead of ~4 MB;
  • the reference was chr6-only, so KIR on chr19 could never match.

Script 63 fixes this in two stages. First, candidate contigs come from a ±1 Mb slice. Second, those contigs are confirmed against the whole primary genome at MAPQ ≥ 20, ≥ 10 kb blocks, with a 25 MB size guard. Calls are then made with the same caller and settings as production, and 17 tests pass. Read-only checks on the VM confirmed the diagnosis: all 991 are Sequel II and were never attempted, their FASTAs are plain gzip (20 of 20 checked), and Revio's are BGZF.

Part 11Disclosure audit and open items

What was fixed in this sprint

Masking each count of 1–19 on its own is not enough. If a table shows a total and all its parts but one, the masked part is a subtraction away. This sprint added complementary suppression: when exactly one cell of a set tied by a sum is hidden, the next smallest is hidden too, shown as <20*. The rule is now generic in _disclosure.py and covers several kinds of sum:

  • the bins of a stratum;
  • ancestries or platforms vs ALL;
  • kept + removed;
  • cells that are the same people under two names. An example is DQ not_called and evidence incomplete in 62.

58 (PR #23), 59, 62 and 65 (PR #25) use it, with audit tests. Pulls of 58, 59, 62 and 65 were held until they passed.

Known residuals in tables committed before this sprint flagged

  • 50_qc_foundation/dq_ascertainment.tsv: the masked count of one-contig cross-group haplotypes can be recovered from the G1/G2 and G2/G1 rows (30/30 and 34/32 split).
  • 55_structure_expression/structure_expression.tsv: the masked KIR2DL4 0-copy n = the ρ-row n − the 1-copy n − the 2-copy n.
  • 56_phasing_typing_benchmark/refined_sweep.tsv: the mixed-platform rows = ALL − Revio–Revio − Sequel IIe–Sequel IIe.
  • 64_novel_allele_validation/tables/summary_by_stratum.tsv: 39 of 176 ancestry/platform partitions have one masked call count. These are calls, not people; masking at publication is your call.

Recommendation: re-derive these four with the generic rule in the next sprint. They are small, mechanical fixes.
