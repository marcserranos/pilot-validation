Part 7Expression checks

Question. Blood RNA is a second, independent measurement. If the DNA says a person has two copies of a gene, the RNA should usually show more of it than in someone with one copy, and none in someone with zero. Two checks were run: presence/absence against expression (55), and a pilot of allele-specific expression on personal references (53).

From which file, by what process (55 and 53)
Data in
55: All of Us v9 RNA-SeQC gene TPM (whole blood, reads aligned to the standard reference), joined to DNA copy counts from Table 1 and the KIR annotation. 53: 80 unrelated people's RNA FASTQs, plus their own two HLA-A/B/C mRNA sequences from the assembly.
Process
55: count the haplotypes carrying the gene (0, 1, 2), then compare TPM across those groups (Spearman ρ). Only "strict" people are used, whose call is trustworthy: DRB5 in people whose DRB1–DQA1 sits on one contig on both haplotypes, and KIR in people with two complete haplotypes. 53: build a per-person reference from their two alleles of each gene, with every other MHC gene as a decoy. Align RNA reads (minimap2 short-read mode, MAPQ ≥ 10), count reads per allele, and test imbalance with a binomial test. Mapping bias is measured by aligning simulated reads from a perfectly balanced 1:1 mix.
Trade-offs
Standard TPM is computed on a reference that lacks many KIR paralogs, so it validates only genes whose paralogs are present. 53 used read 1 only and kept PCR duplicates, a pilot shortcut. Its exact sampling command was not recorded.
Unit
A person (55); a person × gene (53).
Output
55_structure_expression/structure_expression.tsv; 53_hla_ase_pilot/ase_by_gene_class.tsv
Significance

DRB5 shows that the DNA presence calls are right where the reference lets RNA check them.

The ASE pilot shows that personal references can measure allele imbalance in HLA with negligible mapping bias. That is the foundation for idea #3 (drivers of HLA allele-specific expression), but only after a cohort-scale run, which is a long job.

Part 8KIR: structure first, then the ancestry panel

Question. KIR haplotypes vary in gene content (background), so "gene absent" can mean real biology or a contig that stopped early. Script 51 checked which it is. Script 61 then made the ancestry panel on haplotypes that cannot be broken.

From which file, by what process (51 and 61)
Data in
Immuannot's KIR annotation (GTF) per haplotype: gene names, copies, contigs and positions, plus allele calls. 51 used all 24,513 haplotypes; 61 used the 11,882 unrelated KIR-called people (23,755 haplotypes).
Process
  1. Complete haplotype = all KIR calls on one contig, with both end genes 3DL3 and 3DL2 present. Such a haplotype cannot hide a gene in a gap.
  2. Class every haplotype: complete, multi-contig, or one contig with an open end.
  3. Framework-gene presence per class (51).
  4. Per ancestry, on complete haplotypes only (61): gene frequency, a "B-content" share, top alleles (protein field), and genes per haplotype.
Trade-offs
The complete-only rule removes fragmentation bias, but it can add another: if longer or more repetitive haplotypes (often B-content) break more often, complete haplotypes under-represent them. Completeness is close across ancestries (72–79%), so between-ancestry comparisons are fairly safe, but absolute B-content may be slightly low. For simple carrier frequencies, gene presence on any contig is robust to this and serves as a check.
Unit
Haplotypes. 11,882 unrelated is not the 11,856 of the HLA analyses: the greedy unrelated set is built from a different starting cohort (KIR-called vs HLA-called people).
Output
51_kir_structure_qc/; 61_kir_by_ancestry_panel/vm_2026-10-12/

Significance

This is a KIR-by-ancestry panel from complete long-read haplotypes in ~12,000 people, with alleles. Most published KIR frequencies come from presence/absence typing of smaller, mostly European cohorts.

The strongest contrasts are EAS's A-richness and AFR's missing telomeric B block. Their KIR allele distributions differ more than their gene content does. Both are natural partners for the HLA results, since KIR receptors read HLA class I ligands.
