Part 3Can we trust the allele calls?

Question. When the caller says a person carries HLA-DQB1*06:02, how often is that wrong? There is no truth set for 12,000 people, so error is measured from inheritance, which does not depend on the caller.

  • Two relatives who share a haplotype must carry the same allele at every gene on it.
  • A child's allele must be present in the parent who passed it on.

Either way, a failure means at least one of the two calls is wrong.

The confidence score (56): what each point means
Data in
Table 1 (hla_calls_rich.tsv: one row per person × haplotype × gene, with the contig name, the distance to the closest known allele, warning flags and the platform).
Process
Each call gets one point for each of seven properties that a priori make a call more trustworthy:
  1. it shares a contig with its phasing partner gene;
  2. its haplotype's MHC sits on ≤ 2 contigs;
  3. its distance to the nearest known allele is ≤ the cohort's 75th percentile;
  4. its difference from that allele is not only a homopolymer indel;
  5. the caller raised no template warning;
  6. there was no tie between candidate alleles;
  7. it came from Revio.
The score is 0–7, and a claim can then use only calls at or above a threshold.
Trade-offs
The seven points are equally weighted, chosen before seeing error rates, not fitted. A fitted score (for example a logistic model on trio errors) would separate better but would need held-out trios to be honest.
Unit
A call (one gene on one haplotype in one person).
Output
A score column used by 56b and 57b. The per-call scores stay on the VM.
Relatives check (56b) and trio check (57b): data and processing
Data in
56b: the 569 first-degree pairs from Part 2, both HLA-called. 57b: the 179 trios, with each child gene's parent of origin from k-mers (Part 2, check 3).
Process
56b: per gene, ask "do the two relatives share at least one 2-field allele?". Pairs failing at ≥ 2 classical genes are dropped as probable non-sharers (siblings with no haplotype in common), and the rate of genes with no shared allele is reported. 57b: per child gene with a known parent of origin, ask "is the child's allele among that parent's two alleles?", at 2 fields and at full resolution.
Trade-offs
56b: dropping pairs with ≥ 2 failing genes removes true non-sharers, but also pairs with two real typing errors. That pushes the measured error down. With ~4% error per gene over 8 genes, roughly a quarter of errors sit in such pairs, so the true pair rate could be ~5%, not 4.09%. 57b: only HiFi parents are used, and genes without an assigned parent (10–31%) drop out.
Unit
A pair-gene (56b, n = 6,455 at score 0) or a child gene (57b, n = 3,611). Each compares two calls, so the error per single call is about half of what is shown.
Output
56_phasing_typing_benchmark/refined_sweep.tsv, pair_sharing_distribution.tsv; 57_yak_trioeval/mendel_typing.tsv
Significance

The measurement is collapsed onto one axis: the % of comparisons where two calls that should agree do not. About 4% of pair comparisons fail at 2 fields, so a single call is wrong roughly 2–2.5% of the time, and more for MICA and DRB3. That is low enough for population frequencies (an error spreads thinly over many alleles) but not negligible for rare-allele claims. A rare allele seen in 10 people could be partly made of errors from a common neighbour.

Full resolution is about 3× worse. Any claim at the third or fourth field (synonymous or intronic novelty, expression by exact allele) should carry read support or use the score.

Part 4Coverage over the immune regions

Question. The supervisor asked for a regional coverage filter: flag people whose MHC (or KIR) depth is far below their genome depth, on the theory that thin regions give bad calls. Script 58 measured depth for everyone. Script 59 then asked whether thin coverage explains the calls we distrust, and whether a cutoff would remove a useful share of them.

From which file, by what process (58)
Data in
Each person's HiFi BAM aligned to GRCh38 without ALT contigs, read in place from the bucket (12,233 people). A BED file of 137 regions: the MHC window chr6:29.5–33.5 Mb, the KIR window on chr19, every HLA, KIR and TRB gene (GENCODE v46), and 5 control windows elsewhere in the genome.
Process
  1. samtools bedcov sums read bases per region, at MAPQ ≥ 0 (all reads) and ≥ 20 (confidently placed reads).
  2. Mean depth = bases ÷ region length.
  3. Relative depth = region depth ÷ the median of the person's 5 control windows. So 1.0 means "as covered as the rest of this person's genome".
  4. Summaries by ancestry and platform; TRB per-gene histograms and copy-number calls (cuts 0.25 and 0.75).
Trade-offs
Dividing by the person's own control depth removes differences in sequencing amount between people, so thin means "thin for this person". MAPQ 0 counts reads that could map in several places; MAPQ 20 drops them. Both are shown. KIR genes that GRCh38 carries only on ALT contigs cannot be measured on these BAMs.
Unit
A person × region value. Every figure collapses it to the distribution across people.
Output
58_regional_depth/depth_summary_by_region.tsv, trb_rel_depth_histogram.tsv, trb_cn_by_ancestry.tsv. Per-person depths stay on the VM.

Is thin coverage behind the bad calls? Partly, but no filter helps

From which file, by what process (59)
Data in
58's per-person depths (VM), joined to per-person outcome flags derived from Table 1 and the KIR annotation, for the 11,856 unrelated people.
Process
  1. Normalise each window's relative depth to the cohort median for the same region and platform, so 1.0 = typical for that region.
  2. Flag eight outcomes per person: KIR haplotype incomplete; KIR framework gene missing; an HLA gene absent; an empty haplotype; DQ genes split across contigs; anomalous DQ spacing; a cross-group DQ pair; MHC on ≥ 7 contigs. A negative control is added: framework gene missing on a complete KIR haplotype, which should be biology, not assembly.
  3. Outcome rate per 0.1 depth bin, with Wilson CIs.
  4. Logistic regression per outcome: odds ratio per 0.1 lower depth, adjusted for platform and ancestry.
  5. A cutoff scan under a rule fixed in advance: remove ≥ 50% of an outcome's events while removing ≤ 10% of people, overall and in every ancestry with ≥ 100 people.
Trade-offs
The rule accepts a filter only if it is clearly worth it. It cannot pass for a common outcome: removing 10% of people can remove at most 10/p% of events, where p is the outcome's prevalence. For KIR incomplete (p ≈ 38%) that ceiling is 26%. For common outcomes the cutoff scan therefore cannot decide anything on its own; the logistic odds ratios and the rate-by-bin curves are the real test.
Unit
A person. Rates are % of people in a depth bin with the outcome.
Output
59_depth_outcome_join/vm_2026-10-12/: outcome_by_depth_bin.tsv, logistic_or.tsv, cutoff_scan.tsv, proposed_cutoff.tsv
Significance

Coverage is a structure marker, not a QC knob. The useful consequences:

  • No coverage filter is applied, and 61 and 62 were run with an exclusion list of 0 people.
  • Depth can be used as a covariate in KIR models.
  • A methods paragraph can say exactly this: low depth goes with KIR fragmentation, partly because contracted haplotypes lower depth, and no cutoff removes a useful share of problem calls without removing real biology.
