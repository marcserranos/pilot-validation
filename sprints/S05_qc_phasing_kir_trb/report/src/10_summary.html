S05 Sprint Report

This report covers QC, phasing, KIR and TRB germline genetics in the All of Us long-read cohort. The sprint ran from 6 to 12 October 2026 (scripts 50–65) and answered the supervisor's asks from the 6 October meeting:

  • exact counts;
  • whether the phased haplotypes can be trusted;
  • KIR by ancestry;
  • regional coverage;
  • the TRBV4-3 question.

Every method is explained in the background course, and every result carries its data trail. An independent review checked the work, and its corrections are built in.

The answers, with how sure each one is

Class II and B~C haplotypes on one contig are reliable; anything with HLA-A is not

In 179 trios, fewer than 20 of 248–262 one-contig DQ, DR–DQ, DP and B~C gene pairs mixed DNA from both parents. That puts the rate below ~7.7%; the true value is probably far lower, since the first 50 trios showed 0 of 69–76. Pairs that include HLA-A mixed parents ~40% of the time ().How sure: high for the ranking. The 7.7% is a ceiling forced by masking, not a measured rate, and k-mer parent assignment has its own unmeasured error.

2-field typing disagrees in ~4% of relative checks, so roughly 2% of calls are wrong

Relative pairs and parent–child checks both fail in ~4% of genes (4.09% vs 4.15%). Each check compares two calls, so the per-call error is about half of that. Full resolution (4 fields) fails ~3× more often. A confidence score ≥ 4 cuts the error by 37–50% and keeps 91% of relative pair-genes ().How sure: moderate. It is a lower bound, because errors that keep alleles shared are invisible. The two estimates share people, so they are not fully independent.

The "many novel HLA proteins" headline does not survive

4,250 classical protein-novel sequences shrink to 0 that are recurrent, clean and common. Of the recurrent ones, 4 have read support at non-homopolymer sites, and 2 of those (both DPB1) have since been named in IPD-IMGT/HLA. That leaves 2 still-unnamed candidates with clean read support, or 7 if homopolymer-class support is accepted. In 345 calls, the caller's two coding-sequence outputs disagree by a frame-changing indel, source undetermined ().How sure: high that the headline fails. The read evidence comes from the same HiFi reads that built the assembly, so it is support, not independent proof. The 2 DPB1 alleles named later are a good sign that the pipeline finds real alleles.

No cross-group DQ haplotype exists on intact chromosomes

Cross-group DQA1~DQB1 pairs appear almost only across contig breaks, and the few on one contig are structurally abnormal. The haplotype atlas finds the DQ and DR51/52/53 rules without being told them ().How sure: high for "no evidence of cross-group haplotypes". This shows absence, not the folding biology behind it.

Coverage is not a usable filter, but it is not irrelevant

The MHC and KIR run 12–17% below genome depth in nearly everyone. Lower depth goes with more fragmented KIR (34% → 55%), partly because haplotypes with fewer genes give lower depth. No cutoff removes a useful share of bad calls, so no filter is applied ().Corrected after review: the first draft said depth "explains none". That conclusion was guaranteed by the rule's arithmetic.

TRBV4-3 absence is common and ancestry-structured, but the repertoire numbers don't add up yet

No germline copy: EUR 19.5%, AMR 30.1%, MID ~32%, EAS 37.1%, AFR 38.5%, SAS 55.1%. The ranking matches who never uses the gene, in the same order in all six groups. But fewer people never use it (27%) than lack it (33%), which cannot happen if both measures were exact. The repertoire count is a known lower bound, and some 0-copy calls may be wrong ().How sure: high for the ranking, moderate for the exact AFR and SAS rates. "Consistent with the deletion driving non-use" is as far as the data go; proving it needs the per-person test (script 60), which waits on Aleix.

Decisions only Marc can make

Each item is a proposal with a recommendation; items 1–4 are also open entries in context/DECISIONS.md. The sprint's code supports every option. The consequence of saying no is given too.

  1. Ancestry-purity rule. Recommended: run the primary analyses on predicted ancestry, with p ≥ 0.95 as a sensitivity analysis. If you require p ≥ 0.95 everywhere instead: EUR and MID drop to 38% of their people, while SAS keeps 93% ().
  2. Demote classical protein-novel counts to a resource table, and name only the read-supported candidates (2 unnamed plus 2 since named in IPD-IMGT/HLA). Recommended: yes. If no: a reviewer can show that most of the counts are homopolymer artefacts.
  3. KIR haplotype claims only on complete haplotypes (75%), with gene presence also reported from any contig as a check. Recommended: yes. If no: broken contigs pass for absent genes.
  4. Physical-haplotype rule. DQ, DR–DQ, DP and B~C may be used from one contig. A~B and A~C need statistical phasing or trios. DQ–DP is flagged. Recommended: yes. Cole's units (62) already follow it.
  5. No coverage filter. Accept that depth is a structure marker, not a usable QC cut. Recommended: yes.
  6. TRB genotyper. Switch from fixed cuts to the new mixture model, which allows for the read-capture bias, once 65b checks it against assemblies. On the full cohort it leaves the 0-copy rates unchanged and removes the heterozygote deficit everywhere except AFR (and SAS, untestable in the public table). Recommended: yes.
  7. Long jobs (Sequel II re-call, full ASE, genome-wide trio phasing). They are planned and costed in Long jobs, for you to discuss. None was run.
  8. Small allele counts. Counts of distinct allele sequences below 20 (for example "4 candidates") count alleles, not people, so they are shown exactly. Decide whether to mask them in published figures.
