Part 9The TRB germline insertion and TRBV4-3

Question. Aleix found that 27.3% of people never use TRBV4-3 in their T-cell repertoire, from SAS 43.5% down to EUR 16.7%. A known germline polymorphism, an optional ~21.7 kb insertion, carries TRBV4-3 together with TRBV3-2 and a second copy of TRBV6-2/6-3 (background). A person without the insertion on either chromosome cannot use TRBV4-3. How common is that, by ancestry? Does it account for the usage gap?

The deletion itself is in the literature. What would be new is ancestry-stratified long-read genotyping in ~11,000 diverse people, and, once script 60 runs, a per-person link to the same people's repertoires.

How each person was genotyped (65, reads mode), and the bias this created
Data in
Each person's HiFi reads over the TRB V cluster, read in place from the BAM (12,233 people), and the IMGT TRBV4-3 / TRBV3-2 probe sequences.
Process
  1. A reference read runs across the empty insertion site without a break: it covers the whole ~5.9 kb breakpoint interval plus ≥ 1 kb anchored on each side, ~7.9 kb in total. Only a chromosome without the insertion can produce one.
  2. An insertion read carries a TRBV4-3 (or TRBV3-2) probe hit at ≥ 97% identity. Only a chromosome with the insertion can produce one.
  3. Allele fraction (AF) = insertion reads ÷ (insertion + reference reads).
  4. Genotype by fixed cuts: 0 copies below 0.2, 1 copy from 0.2 to 0.8, 2 copies above 0.8. At least 6 informative reads are required (median 14).
Trade-offs
The two read types are not equally easy to get. A read of length L can contain the ~0.3 kb probe from about L + 0.3 kb of start positions, but can span the 7.9 kb reference requirement from only about L − 7.9 kb. For typical HiFi reads (L ≈ 18 kb) that is 18.3 vs 10.1, a 1.8:1 ratio, so a true heterozygote is expected at AF ≈ 1.8 ÷ 2.8 ≈ 0.64, not 0.5. The bias also changes with each person's read lengths, so no single fixed cut is right for everyone.
Unit
A person (genotype); allele frequency counts chromosomes.
Output
65_trb_assembly_genotype/reads_unrelated/ and reads_all/: genotype_by_stratum.tsv, allele_fraction_histogram.tsv

The fix: a genotyper that learns where heterozygotes sit

Instead of fixed cuts, the new --genotyper mixture fits three groups (0, 1, 2 copies) to each feature and platform at once. Each group has its own expected AF, so the het mode is learned (about 0.63 here) instead of assumed to be 0.5. It is a binomial mixture fitted by EM; a person is called only if their most likely group has ≥ 90% posterior probability, and otherwise is "ambiguous". It is written and tested (27 tests pass), and needs a VM run of a few minutes in aggregate mode.

Result on the whole cohort (13 Oct, VM re-aggregation of the stored per-person read counts). The fitted read fraction for 1 copy is 0.637–0.641 for both TRBV4-3 and TRBV3-2 on both platforms, as the read rules predict (0.64). The 0-copy and 2-copy groups sit at about 0.00 and 1.00. Only 0.3–1.0% of people are left ambiguous; 93.1% of the unrelated people get a TRBV4-3 call.

  • The 0-copy rates do not move: EUR 19.5%, AMR 30.2%, EAS 37.2%, AFR 38.6%, SAS 55.2%, all within 0.1 point of the fixed cuts. The chart below (fixed cuts) therefore stands.
  • The insertion frequency drops (EUR 0.549, AMR 0.450, EAS and AFR 0.393, all ancestries 0.435), because people the cuts called 2 copies are now heterozygotes.
  • The heterozygote deficit is gone in AMR, EAS and EUR (Hardy–Weinberg p = 1.0, 0.61 and 0.10). AFR keeps a deficit (p = 2.7×10−4; about 1,220 heterozygotes observed vs ~1,310 expected, F ≈ 0.07). SAS cannot be tested from the public table, because masking hides its 1-copy/2-copy split; the local preview had F ≈ 0.10.

Two explanations for AFR and SAS are open. The insertion alleles there may be divergent enough to miss the 97%-identity probes, so heterozygotes would be called 0. Or there is real structure within those labels (several sub-populations with different frequencies pooled together, which always produces excess homozygotes). The 65b assembly comparison, stratified by ancestry, can tell these apart.

Why the bounds matter: 0-copy people have only the harder-to-capture reference reads, so they are more likely to fall under the 6-read floor and go uncalled. Call rates are lowest where the deletion is commonest (AFR, SAS, and Sequel IIe at 87.9%). The truth is probably in the upper half of each range.

Significance

The axis is % of people with zero germline copies, set against % of people with zero repertoire use. The ranking agreement across six ancestries is strong evidence that the deletion matters. TRBV4-3 non-use in SAS, the highest of any group, is mostly explained by germline absence: 55% of SAS lack the gene entirely.

For any TCR study, TRBV4-3 usage differences between populations must be adjusted for germline copy number before being read as immunology. The per-person test (60) turns this from "consistent with" into a measured effect.

Part 10The 991 Sequel II people (mostly AFR)

Question. Why do 991 people, about a quarter of AFR participants, have no HLA or KIR calls, and what would it take to call them?

Diagnosis and fix (63)
Data in
The long-read manifest and each person's assembly FASTA (SEQUEL2_DIAGNOSIS.md).
Process
Production extracts the MHC and KIR contigs from each assembly using the assembly-to-GRCh38 alignment. The Sequel II people have no such alignment, so they fell into a "self-align" tier that production skipped. That path was also broken in three ways:
  • their FASTAs are plain gzip, which samtools faidx cannot index;
  • a whole assembly aligned to chr6 alone pulled in 1.3–1.9 GB per haplotype instead of ~4 MB;
  • the reference was chr6-only, so KIR on chr19 could never match.
Script 63 fixes it in two stages. Stage 1 finds candidate contigs against a ±1 Mb slice of the reference. Stage 2 confirms them against the whole primary genome at MAPQ ≥ 20, with ≥ 10 kb blocks and a 25 MB size guard. Calls are then made with the same caller and settings as production.
Trade-offs
Self-alignment uses a different extraction route from production, so before pooling, ~20 Revio people should be run through it and compared with their production calls.
Status
The first 2-person smoke test stopped at the 25 MB guard, which did its job. Long repeat chains at low identity had passed stage 1 (in one haplotype, 17,011 of 17,053 blocks were below 0.8 identity, pulling in ~10,000 contigs). The real MHC contigs sit at 0.95–0.98 identity (42 contigs, 6.1 MB). Stage 1 now also requires block identity ≥ 0.8 (merged, with a test). The second smoke test (13 Oct) passed: 2 people, all 4 person-locus jobs worked, and all 8 haplotypes were confirmed in stage 2. HLA candidate sets were 4.2–6.1 MB per haplotype and KIR about 1.8 MB, the expected sizes. It took 0.70 h in total, about 19–23 min per person, half the earlier estimate. Peak memory was not recorded; the 20-person Revio comparison will measure it.
Significance

Every AFR frequency in this report rests on 3,020 unrelated people instead of ~4,000. If the Sequel II AFR people differ from the Revio AFR people (another site, another time), estimates may be biased too.

The re-call is the single job with the largest effect on the AFR results. It is also more than a day of compute, so it is planned, not run (see the next section).
