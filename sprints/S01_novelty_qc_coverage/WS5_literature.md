## C. Coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation (Chao & Jost 2012, iNEXT, Good-Turing) — formulas and genomics uses

1. **Chao A, Jost L. "Coverage-based rarefaction and extrapolation: standardizing samples by completeness rather than size."** Ecology, 2012, 93(12):2533-2547. DOI 10.1890/11-1952.1. URL: https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/11-1952.1 ; PubMed 23431585.
   - Core idea directly usable for our discovery-curve/coverage framing: propose standardizing comparisons across samples by **sample coverage** (estimated completeness) rather than raw sample size N, because a fixed N under- or over-samples depending on the true richness/diversity of the underlying assemblage — exactly our situation comparing ancestries with different underlying HLA allele richness.
   - **Formula (Good-Turing / Chao estimator of sample coverage), corroborated across multiple secondary sources (iNEXT documentation, MetricGate, entropart R package) but NOT verified by direct fetch of the primary PDF this session — mark as UNVERIFIED at the primary-source level, high-confidence via convergent secondary sources:**
     - Basic Good-Turing: Ĉ = 1 − (Q1 / n), where Q1 = number of singletons (alleles/species observed exactly once) and n = total sample size (individuals or reads).
     - **Chao-corrected ("Chao & Jost") estimator using both singletons and doubletons:**
       Ĉ = 1 − (Q1/n) · [ (n−1)Q1 / ((n−1)Q1 + 2·Q2) ]
       where Q2 = number of doubletons (observed exactly twice). This down-weights the naive Good-Turing correction when doubletons are also rare, which is the regime relevant to rare HLA alleles.
     - Sample-size-to-target-coverage extrapolation: Chao & Jost's companion approach (implemented in **iNEXT**, Hsieh, Ma & Chao, "iNEXT: an R package for rarefaction and extrapolation of species diversity (Hill numbers)," Methods in Ecology and Evolution, 2016, DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.12613) extrapolates the rarefaction/extrapolation curve of observed vs. estimated-asymptotic richness to solve for the sample size n* at which estimated coverage reaches a target Ĉ (e.g. 0.95, 0.99) — recommended practice is extrapolation to at most double the reference sample size for reliable inference (this "double the sample size" rule of thumb is explicitly stated in Chao & Jost 2012 per secondary-source summaries). Exact closed-form extrapolation formula (their eq. for coverage-based extrapolated richness) not independently re-derived here — UNVERIFIED at formula-level fidelity; recommend a direct read of the Ecology paper's Table 1/eq 4-9 before quoting formulas in a manuscript.

2. **iNEXT package**: Hsieh TC, Ma KH, Chao A. "iNEXT: an R package for rarefaction and extrapolation of species diversity (Hill numbers)." Methods in Ecology and Evolution, 2016, 7(12):1451-1456. https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12613 — implements the above; directly usable/citable as the software for our own coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation curves per ancestry group.

3. **Chiu CH et al., "Sample coverage estimation, rarefaction, and extrapolation based on sample-based abundance data,"** Ecology, 2023, DOI 10.1002/ecy.4099 — a 2023 extension to sample-based (incidence) abundance data; relevant if our per-individual (rather than per-allele-copy) sampling scheme is better modeled as incidence data rather than raw abundance. UNVERIFIED beyond abstract/title.

4. **Applications in immunogenetics/human genetics:**
   - **TCR repertoire diversity**: multiple papers apply Good-Turing/Chao-family richness estimators (Chao1, Chao2, ACE, bootstrap, Good-Turing) to unseen-clonotype estimation, e.g. Laydon DJ et al. "Estimating T-cell repertoire diversity: limitations of classical estimators and a new approach," PMC4528489 (Philos Trans R Soc B, ~2015 — year UNVERIFIED from snippet); and a 2025 review "A comprehensive evaluation of diversity measures for TCR repertoire profiling," PubMed 40369611 (venue/year UNVERIFIED beyond PMID, appears 2025).    - **"How many genomes are needed" variant-discovery framing**: no single canonical Chao-style paper found for HLA specifically, but the closest analog is gnomAD/HPRC diversity-over-size arguments (see section D) and a methods paper "Predicting discovery rates of genomic features" (arXiv 1403.3414) — UNVERIFIED beyond title/arXiv listing, flagged for follow-up if directly relevant.
   - GAP: no source found this session directly applying Chao & Jost coverage-based rarefaction/extrapolation to **HLA allele discovery** specifically — this appears to be an actual methodological gap/opportunity for our paper (i.e., we may be first to apply coverage-based rarefaction explicitly to HLA allele-space discovery curves). ## D. Reference-panel design / "how many genomes from which ancestry" arguments in human genomics

1. **"A harmonized public resource of deeply sequenced diverse human genomes"** (gnomAD-HGDP+1KGP paper), bioRxiv 2023.01.23.525248 / PMC9900804 (published version: likely Genome Research or similar — venue UNVERIFIED beyond PMCID/bioRxiv). Relevant: explicit "diversity over sample size" argument — with ~76,156-140,000+ genomes in gnomAD, each newly sequenced individual still carries ~200 very rare coding variants and tens of variants entirely absent from the database, i.e. discovery is NOT saturated even at gnomAD's scale; strongly analogous framing to our "allele-space discovery curves not saturated" finding for HLA.

2. **"Diverse ancestral representation improves genetic intolerance metrics"**, PMC11920395 (journal/year UNVERIFIED beyond PMCID, likely 2024-2025). Directly supports the core reference-panel-design argument we want to cite: a **gene-intolerance metric (MTR) trained on only ~43,000 multi-ancestry exomes outperformed the same metric trained on a nearly 10x larger (440,000) non-Finnish-European-only exome set** — i.e., ancestral diversity has a bigger marginal effect on discovery/utility than raw sample size. This is a strong, quotable, directly analogous precedent for arguing that ancestry-balanced (not just larger) HLA cohorts are needed to close IPD-IMGT/HLA gaps.

3. HPRC's own diversity rationale (see section A refs) — the original HPRC draft pangenome paper ("A Draft Human Pangenome Reference," Nature, 2023, Wang T et al., https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05896-x — note: search returned a 2022 bioRxiv preprint at 10.1101/2022.07.09.499321v1; the Nature-published version year/DOI UNVERIFIED this session, should confirm exact citation before use) explicitly argues for ancestral diversity of assembled genomes over raw genome count to capture structural/pangenome variation — same rhetorical structure as our argument for HLA.

GAP: no source found this session that models, with an explicit statistical/formal framework (e.g., a Chao-style extrapolation), how many genomes *of a given ancestry* are needed to reach a target discovery/coverage fraction for any genomic feature class (SNPs, SVs, or HLA alleles) — most reference-panel-design papers make the diversity-over-size argument qualitatively/empirically (via held-out discovery curves) rather than with a closed-form coverage estimator. This reinforces the section-C gap: combining Chao-Jost coverage formalism with ancestry-stratified genomics reference-panel design appears to be an open combination, supporting a novelty claim for our WS on this specific methodological pairing (formal coverage estimation applied to ancestry-stratified HLA discovery).

## E. Validation standards for novel HLA alleles from long reads; MHC assembly failure modes

**Validation standards:**

1. **"Identification of the Novel HLA-A*02:04:03 Allele by Long-Read Sequencing Following a Short-Read Phasing Failure,"** HLA (journal), 2026 (Wiley, DOI 10.1111/tan.70961; also PMC13527173). URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/tan.70961
   - Relevant case study: novel allele missed by short-read phasing, recovered by long-read sequencing; reported with average 261x coverage and well-balanced allele ratios as supporting evidence — a concrete precedent for what "adequate read support" looks like in a single-sample HLA novel-allele report (much higher per-sample depth than our presumably lower average coverage across ~12,000 samples — worth flagging as a QC contrast).

2. **"Four Novel HLA Alleles, Confirmed by Sanger Dideoxy Nucleotide Sequencing,"** PMC13420740 / PubMed 42528172 (journal/year UNVERIFIED beyond PMCID/PMID — appears to be a 2026 case-report-style paper based on ID range).
   - States the field-standard confirmation protocol: novel alleles confirmed by Sanger sequencing (exons 2-4 amplified by PCR, sequenced on ABI 3500xl) — i.e., **orthogonal Sanger confirmation of the discriminating exons is still treated as the expected/gold-standard bar** for individual novel-allele case reports, which is a much higher per-allele validation bar than population-scale computational pipelines (like ours) can apply to every novel call. This is an important limitation to state explicitly in our paper: our novelty calls are read-support/QC-filtered but not individually Sanger-confirmed at the ~thousands-of-alleles scale, which HLA nomenclature convention would ideally want.
   - General statement corroborated by review context: "putative novel alleles identified by long-read sequencing should be confirmed by Sanger sequencing of the relevant exons or full gene before official database submission."

3. UNVERIFIED whether any HLA-specific study has applied this specifically to novel-allele confirmation (found in general long-read benchmarking context, not HLA-specific, from search snippet only).

**MHC/long-read assembly failure modes:**

4. **"Overcoming uncollapsed haplotypes in long-read assemblies of non-model organisms,"** BMC Bioinformatics, 2021, DOI 10.1186/s12859-021-04118-3 (also PMC8178825, bioRxiv 2020.03.16.993428).
